# Narrative Codebook — Tieba Elder-Scam Incidents (Final Plateau Edition)

**Scope:** Apply to Baidu Tieba posts that describe, discuss, question, warn about, or otherwise concern scams affecting older adults (post title + body + comment excerpts). Each numbered item below is a coding variable. Closed-list variables: pick from the list — never invent, rename, or translate a category; copy code names exactly as written. Multi-select variables: code all that apply, within the stated limits. When a variable does not apply to the post, code "N/A" where the list offers it.

Coding is based on the whole post: title, body, and the comment excerpts. Judge only what the post evidences — do not speculate beyond the text.

## 1. Scam Relevance (single-select)

- Yes (Scam-Relevant)
- Unknown (Undetermined)
- No (Not a Scam)

Code exactly one value for every post; this variable is never left blank and never takes multiple codes.

Note: The split between Unknown (Undetermined) and No (Not a Scam) is epistemic vs. substantive. Unknown means the coder cannot tell whether deception occurred (typically 求鉴定 posts whose own question stays unresolved); No means the coder positively concluded there is no scam. If the post asks "is this a scam?" and the facts do not settle it, code Unknown, not No.

Definitions:

- **Yes (Scam-Relevant):** The coder affirmatively judges that the post describes, warns about, or educates on an elder scam or elder-targeted fraud. This includes victim and family accounts, news reports, public warnings, and PSA/科普 posts about elder-directed scams (保健品, 传销, 存款变保单, investment fraud, etc.), per the project's loose relevance criterion. Borderline-but-plausible cases are coded plain Yes under the no-borderline policy. Distinguish from Unknown (Undetermined): Yes requires the coder to have positively concluded that deception occurred or is described.

- **Unknown (Undetermined):** The coder cannot determine from the post whether a scam actually occurred — typically 求鉴定 / seeking-verification posts, ambiguous sales situations, or incidents with too little detail to establish deceit. This code records the coder's uncertainty, not a judgment about the incident itself. Distinguish from No (Not a Scam): if the post's own "is this a scam?" question remains unresolved, the label is Unknown, not No.

- **No (Not a Scam):** The coder concluded on inspection that the post does not describe a scam — for example an ordinary commercial dispute, a legitimate product, or an incident with no deception. This is an affirmative negative judgment, not an inability to decide.

## 2. Purpose of Post (single-select)

- Help & Advice Seeking
- Public Warning & Education
- Complaint & Grievance
- News & Media Exposure
- Verification & Appraisal Seeking
- Personal Narrative & Testimony
- Promotion & Testimonial
- Public Appeal & Mobilization
- Discussion & Commentary

Assign exactly one code to every post: pick the single dominant communicative purpose — what the poster is primarily trying to accomplish. Every post in the sample takes exactly one code; this variable is never left N/A.

Note: Several codes sit close together and are split by the primary ask. If the poster wants a course of action from forum peers, code Help & Advice Seeking; if they want a factual verdict ("is it a scam / is it real?"), code Verification & Appraisal Seeking; if they want redress from a named party, code Complaint & Grievance; if they want authorities, media, or the public to intervene, code Public Appeal & Mobilization. Content composed by the poster to protect readers is Public Warning & Education; relayed third-party journalism is News & Media Exposure; a recounting with no dominant ask is Personal Narrative & Testimony.

Definitions:

- **Help & Advice Seeking:** The poster asks fellow forum users for assistance with a concrete scam situation — what to do, how to intervene with a scammed parent, how to recover money, or simply emotional support in coping (求助/求安慰). Distinguish from Verification & Appraisal Seeking: the ask here is practical (a course of action), not epistemic (a verdict). Distinguish from Public Appeal & Mobilization: the appeal is to forum peers, not to authorities or media.

- **Public Warning & Education:** The poster's own composition alerting other users to a scam pattern, venue, or company so they and their elders can avoid it, including didactic 科普 scam-awareness write-ups and victims warning others from experience. Distinguish from Complaint & Grievance: a warning aims to protect readers, a complaint aims at redress against a named party. Distinguish from News & Media Exposure: a warning is authored by the poster; news posts relay third-party journalism.

- **Complaint & Grievance:** The poster airs a grievance against a named seller, company, platform, or institution over a scam or deceptive sale, seeking refund, redress, or public accountability (维权-style), whether first-party or on behalf of a family member. Distinguish from Personal Narrative & Testimony: a complaint carries a primary demand for accountability; a narrative recounts events without one.

- **News & Media Exposure:** The post relays third-party coverage of elder-scam cases — reposted news articles, investigative exposés, or reports of legal proceedings, arrests, and verdicts. Distinguish from Public Warning & Education: the poster shares coverage rather than composing an alert, even if warning intent is implicit. Distinguish from Personal Narrative & Testimony: relayed journalism, not the poster's own experience.

- **Verification & Appraisal Seeking:** The poster asks the community to judge whether a product, offer, company, or claim is genuine or a scam, or to appraise an item's authenticity or value (求鉴定). Distinguish from Help & Advice Seeking: the ask is for a factual verdict, not for advice on what to do.

- **Personal Narrative & Testimony:** A first- or second-hand recounting of a scam experience — what happened to the poster, their family, or someone they observed — with narration dominating and no single primary ask. Distinguish from Complaint & Grievance (no redress demand), from Public Warning & Education (no explicit warning purpose, even if readers may take a lesson), and from News & Media Exposure (own experience vs. relayed journalism).

- **Promotion & Testimonial:** The post promotes, defends, or gives a positive testimonial for a product, company, treatment, service, or opportunity — frequently the scam content itself (shill, MLM recruitment, corporate defense, or a solicitor advertising services to victims). This is the in-the-wild scam-content genre, authored from the seller/believer side rather than the victim/observer side.

- **Public Appeal & Mobilization:** The poster calls on authorities, media, or the public at large to act on a scam case — demanding arrests, collective 维权, or journalistic attention. Distinguish from Help & Advice Seeking (addressed to powerful third parties for intervention, not to forum peers for advice) and from Complaint & Grievance (mobilizes collective or institutional action beyond the poster's own redress).

- **Discussion & Commentary:** An open-ended prompt for community discussion or general social commentary on elder-scam phenomena (e.g., whether one dares help a fallen elder given 碰瓷), not tied to seeking help for one concrete case. Distinguish from Public Warning & Education: it invites opinions and deliberation rather than issuing an alert.

## 3. Category of Scam (single or up to two)

- MLM & Pyramid Schemes
- Health Product & Supplement Sales
- Investment & Illegal Fundraising
- Deceptive & Overpriced Product Sales
- Insurance & Deposit Mis-selling
- Fake Medicine & Miracle-Cure Treatment
- Collectibles, Antiques & Jewelry Scams
- Health & Therapy Device Scams
- Telecom & Phone-Service Billing Scams
- Impersonation Scams
- Property, Mortgage & Loan Fraud
- Tourism & Forced-Shopping Scams
- Romance & Companionship Scams
- Prize & Lucky-Draw Scams
- Elder Financial Abuse & Theft
- Extortion & Staged-Incident Coercion
- Fee-for-Service & Care Fraud
- Superstition & Religious Scams
- Digital Account, Data & Marketplace Fraud
- Other / Mixed Confidence Scams
- N/A

Code the scheme by its mechanism and merchandise/asset domain. Assign one code unless the post genuinely fuses two distinct mechanisms or a mechanism plus a second merchandise domain (e.g. 保健品 sold through 传销 recruitment, jade sold at a forced tour stop). Never assign more than two.

Note: Three codes cover the "health" economy and are split by what is bought: ingestible supplements (保健品) go to Health Product & Supplement Sales, medicines/treatment courses for a named disease go to Fake Medicine & Miracle-Cure Treatment, and durable apparatus (理疗仪, massage chairs) goes to Health & Therapy Device Scams. MLM & Pyramid Schemes vs Investment & Illegal Fundraising turns on where returns come from: recruiting a downline vs depositing capital for promised interest. Collectibles, Antiques & Jewelry Scams vs Deceptive & Overpriced Product Sales turns on whether value rests on claimed authenticity/rarity/appreciation vs an ordinary consumer good simply misrepresented or overpriced.

Definitions:

- **MLM & Pyramid Schemes:** Recruitment-driven schemes (传销/直销) where money is made through entry fees, forced product purchases, and building a downline — including named Chinese schemes and consumption-rebate platforms. Distinguish from Investment & Illegal Fundraising: here the elder is recruited into a hierarchy, not merely depositing funds for interest; and from Health Product & Supplement Sales: the elder becomes a distributor/recruiter, not just a customer.
- **Health Product & Supplement Sales:** Deceptive marketing and sale of health foods and supplements (保健品) to elders — free-gift seminars (会销), telemarketing, testimonial articles, livestreams, TV ads — on exaggerated or fabricated health benefits. Distinguish from Fake Medicine & Miracle-Cure Treatment: the goods are positioned as wellness supplements, not medicines or treatment for a named disease.
- **Investment & Illegal Fundraising:** Schemes soliciting elders' savings on promises of high or guaranteed returns — P2P lending, 非法集资, Ponzi-style pension products, fake original shares/pre-IPO stock, fake trading platforms, collectible-as-investment pitches. Distinguish from MLM & Pyramid Schemes: returns are promised on deposited capital, not on recruiting; and from Insurance & Deposit Mis-selling: no regulated insurance/deposit product is being misrepresented.
- **Deceptive & Overpriced Product Sales:** The residual "goods" code: ordinary consumer products (water purifiers, phones, liquor, watches, cleaning products) sold to elders counterfeit, drastically overpriced, or not as described, via TV shopping, livestreams, door-to-door visits, or free-gift bait. Distinguish from the three health codes: the product carries no medical/health-cure framing; and from Collectibles, Antiques & Jewelry Scams: the goods are utilitarian items with no authenticity or appreciation claim.
- **Insurance & Deposit Mis-selling:** Mis-selling of insurance and bank products to elders — classically 存款变保单, where an intended bank-lobby deposit is converted into an insurance policy through verbal promises contradicting the contract — plus fake policies and bad-faith claim denial. Distinguish from Investment & Illegal Fundraising: the vehicle is a real (or purportedly real) regulated financial product misrepresented at the point of sale, not an unlicensed fundraising scheme.
- **Fake Medicine & Miracle-Cure Treatment:** Sale of fake, unlicensed, or unproven medicines and medical/dental treatment courses promising to cure serious or chronic diseases (cancer, diabetes, heart disease), including questionable clinics and dangerous regimens. Distinguish from Health Product & Supplement Sales: the offer is a medicine or treatment for a named disease, not general wellness; and from Health & Therapy Device Scams: the cure is a drug or procedure, not a physical device.
- **Collectibles, Antiques & Jewelry Scams:** Sale of fake, counterfeit, or wildly overpriced collectibles, antiques, commemorative coins, stamps, jade, and jewelry, traded on false authenticity certificates, subsidy claims, or promised appreciation and auction resale. Distinguish from Investment & Illegal Fundraising: the elder receives a physical object rather than depositing capital in a scheme.
- **Health & Therapy Device Scams:** Sale of overpriced or pseudoscientific health apparatus — electrotherapy/high-voltage chairs, massage chairs, oxygen machines, magnetized-water devices — typically via free 体验店 trial sessions or device seminars. Distinguish from the other health codes: the purchase is a durable device, not a consumable or a treatment course, and the signature contact pattern is repeated free in-store sessions.
- **Telecom & Phone-Service Billing Scams:** Overcharging and deceptive selling of telecom services — unauthorized value-added subscriptions and deductions (乱扣费), misleading bundled-plan sales, prepaid-airtime bait, premium-rate call-fee traps. Distinguish from Deceptive & Overpriced Product Sales: the loss is recurring service charges/airtime, not a one-time goods purchase; and from Impersonation Scams: the seller is a real (if predatory) carrier or agent, not someone posing as another entity.
- **Impersonation Scams:** The perpetrator poses as someone else — 公检法 (police/court/government), a financial-service or utility employee, or a relative in an emergency — to induce transfers, payments, or loans; assign when the false identity is the core lever of compliance. Distinguish from Romance & Companionship Scams: an existing authority/kin identity is borrowed rather than a new intimate persona built over time; and from Elder Financial Abuse & Theft: the perpetrator's identity is false, not a real trusted role.
- **Property, Mortgage & Loan Fraud:** Schemes that strip elders of housing assets or saddle them with debt — 以房养老 home-equity "pension" schemes, induced mortgages, deceptive property transfers and notarizations, fabricated loan applications, fraudulent personal borrowing. Assign when the extracted asset is real property, a mortgage, or debt in the elder's name; distinguish from Investment & Illegal Fundraising, where the elder hands over liquid savings.
- **Tourism & Forced-Shopping Scams:** Scams built around organized travel — ultra-low-price tours (低价游) recouped through mandatory shopping stops, coerced extra fees, and staged VIP sales of overpriced goods to elderly tourists. Assign when the tour setting is constitutive of the scheme; distinguish from plain Deceptive & Overpriced Product Sales by the captive-audience coercion of a tour.
- **Romance & Companionship Scams:** Exploitation of elders' desire for romance, marriage, or companionship — catfishing, marriage fraud, parasocial livestream grooming, romance used as a channel to sell products or extract money. Distinguish from Impersonation Scams: the scammer builds a new intimate persona rather than claiming an existing authority or kin identity.
- **Prize & Lucky-Draw Scams:** A prize, lottery win, raffle coupon, or quiz-winnings hook draws the elder into paying — discounted "prize" jewelry, substituted prize goods, premium-fee quiz lines. Assign when an unexpected win or discount entitlement is the entry hook; distinguish from Collectibles, Antiques & Jewelry Scams, where the lure is the object's claimed authenticity or appreciation rather than luck.
- **Elder Financial Abuse & Theft:** Direct taking of an elder's money or property by people with real access or trusted standing — family members, insiders/employees, or befrienders — rather than through a deceptive commercial offer. Distinguish from every sales/investment code: no offer is genuinely bargained, assets are simply taken or diverted; and from Impersonation Scams: the perpetrator's real identity or relationship supplies the access.
- **Extortion & Staged-Incident Coercion:** Money extracted through threat, blackmail, or staged incidents — sextortion after staged intimacy, fabricated-injury compensation demands (碰瓷), coerced fees under captive-situation pressure. Distinguish from Impersonation Scams' arrest threats: here the leverage is blackmail material or a manufactured liability, not a false official identity.
- **Fee-for-Service & Care Fraud:** Escalating or fraudulent fees charged for promised professional or care services — legal representation sold on promised case outcomes, care institutions inflating charges after admission. Distinguish from Fake Medicine & Miracle-Cure Treatment: the service is legal/custodial, not medical; the extraction is service fees against a promised outcome, not a financial product.
- **Superstition & Religious Scams:** Payments and ritual participation extracted through elders' religious faith or superstition — divine commands, temple-building merit, bad-luck removal (消灾). Assign when supernatural authority or fear is the compliance lever; distinguish from Impersonation Scams: the claimed authority is spiritual, not an institutional or kin identity.
- **Digital Account, Data & Marketplace Fraud:** Technology-mediated fraud against elders' devices, data, or online transactions — pre-installed phone malware harvesting personal/SIM data, and fake buyers defrauding elder sellers on online marketplaces. Distinguish from Telecom & Phone-Service Billing Scams: the harm is data compromise or transaction fraud, not service overcharging; here the elder is the seller or the device is the attack vector.
- **Other / Mixed Confidence Scams:** Residual code for posts describing multiple distinct confidence scams against the same elder(s) that cannot be reduced to one scheme type. Use only when the account itself refuses a single scheme; if the identifiable scheme types number two, code those two instead.
- **N/A:** Leave this variable N/A when the post is elder-scam-related but describes no identifiable scam scheme against an elder — e.g. general discussion or PSA threads (should you help a fallen elder given 碰瓷 fears) with no concrete scheme, victim, or transaction to categorize. Do not use N/A to dodge a hard choice between two close codes.

## 4. Promise / Need Exploited (single or multi-select (up to three))

- Cure & Disease Treatment
- Health & Wellness Enhancement
- Investment Gain
- Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts
- Income & Business Opportunity
- Collectible & Valuables Appreciation
- Safe Savings & Principal Security
- Device & Telecom Deal
- Pension & Elder-Care Security
- Prize, Subsidy & Windfall
- Companionship & Romance
- Insurance Protection & Payout
- Helping & Family Emergency
- Authority Compliance & Threat Avoidance
- Problem-Resolution & Recovery Services
- Spiritual & Superstition
- N/A

Code every distinct promise or exploited need in the episode, up to three. Do not add codes merely to fill the cap; use multiple codes only when the text supports distinct promises or needs (for example, free gifts plus health claims).

Note: The two health codes split on whether a named illness is promised cured or controlled (Cure & Disease Treatment) versus generic benefit/prevention/longevity framing (Health & Wellness Enhancement) — 包治百病 bundles frequently earn both. The four money codes split on how the money arrives: profit on placed capital (Investment Gain), deposit-style safe saving (Safe Savings & Principal Security — the 存单变保单 bait), earnings from work/selling/recruiting (Income & Business Opportunity), and explicit coverage/dividend/payout promises (Insurance Protection & Payout). On the shopping side, a purchase-framed deal is Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts, a won or granted windfall is Prize, Subsidy & Windfall, phone/broadband offers always go to Device & Telecom Deal, and object-value claims (jade, antiques, gold) go to Collectible & Valuables Appreciation — discounted-jewelry pitches usually take both Bargain and Collectible.

Definitions:

- **Cure & Disease Treatment:** The scammer promises to cure, treat, control, or relieve a specific disease, symptom, or medical condition — including 包治百病 (cure-all) claims, miracle drugs, unproven therapies (fasting, fire therapy), and medical/dental procedures. Distinguish from Health & Wellness Enhancement: this code requires a claim against an identifiable illness or symptom; vague "health benefits" or prevention stay in Wellness (bundles take both).
- **Health & Wellness Enhancement:** The promise is general health improvement, disease prevention, longevity, immunity, weight loss, beauty, or bodily safety (e.g. safe drinking water from a purifier) without a claim to treat a specific diagnosed illness. Distinguish from Cure & Disease Treatment: once a named disease is promised cured or controlled, code Cure as well or instead. Water-safety promises live here, not in Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts, even when bundled with freebies.
- **Investment Gain:** The promise is profit on money placed into an investment vehicle: high or guaranteed returns, stated interest percentages, dividends, stock/original-share/virtual-currency appreciation, listing profits, P2P or illegal-fundraising yields. Distinguish from Safe Savings & Principal Security: if the elder sought (or was told they were getting) ordinary bank saving, code Savings; profit-seeking framing comes here. Distinguish from Income & Business Opportunity: money earned by working or recruiting is Income, not Investment.
- **Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts:** The promise is exceptional value in an ordinary consumer transaction: deep discounts, free gifts/trials/lectures/checkups (会销 entry hooks), cashback or refunds, quality goods cheap, free or cheap travel — including a buyer's promise to pay the elder for goods. Distinguish from Prize, Subsidy & Windfall: a bargain requires a purchase framing; unearned money or won prizes go to Prize. Phone/broadband offers go to Device & Telecom Deal even when "free."
- **Income & Business Opportunity:** The promise is money earned through work, business, selling, or recruiting others: entrepreneurship pitches, side income, 传销/pyramid earnings, downline-recruitment rewards, 资本运作 (capital-operation) schemes. Distinguish from Investment Gain: income here is earned via active participation, not returns on passively placed capital; hybrid MLM-investment pitches take both.
- **Collectible & Valuables Appreciation:** The promise is that a physical object — jade, jewelry, antiques, stamps, coins, collectible liquor, gold — is authentic, rare, or valuable and will appreciate or resell at a profit; the hallmark 收藏品 scam genre. Distinguish from Investment Gain: the promise centers on a tangible object's worth, not a financial instrument (both named = both coded). Discounted-jewelry pitches are dual-coded with Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts: Bargain holds the deal framing, this code the object-value claim.
- **Safe Savings & Principal Security:** The elder sought — or was told they were getting — safe, liquid, deposit-style saving: bank deposits with modest extra interest, flexible/anytime withdrawal, protected principal. This is a characteristic bait of bancassurance mis-selling (存款变保单). Distinguish from Investment Gain: the framing is safety and saving, not profit-seeking. Distinguish from Insurance Protection & Payout: explicit coverage/dividend/payout promises go to Insurance; deposit-like interest and withdrawal terms stay here even when the product sold was in fact insurance.
- **Device & Telecom Deal:** The promise is a working, cheap, or free phone, computer, or telecom service: elderly phones, free phones with prepaid credit, laptops, broadband and bundled-plan offers, free upgrades. All consumer-electronics and carrier (运营商) offers live here even when framed as free or cheap — do not send them to Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts. Note this is not a tech-support need.
- **Pension & Elder-Care Security:** The promise addresses the elder's 养老 (old-age security) needs: pension income or subsidies, retirement funding, elder-care services, future housing or care, being provided for in later life. Distinguish from Investment Gain: dual-code when a pension promise rides on an investment vehicle — this code captures the old-age-security need, Investment the return promise. Material provision promised via marriage stays here; the emotional connection goes to Companionship & Romance.
- **Prize, Subsidy & Windfall:** The promise is unearned money or a won benefit: lottery/quiz prizes, special-prize draws, government rebates or subsidies, large one-off payouts. Distinguish from Bargain Purchase & Free Gifts: a windfall is presented as won or granted, not as value-for-money in a purchase; prize-coupon retail hooks are dual-coded with Bargain.
- **Companionship & Romance:** The promise is emotional connection: romantic partnership, marriage, friendship or social belonging (e.g. mahjong-partner infiltration), family reunion, or parasocial intimacy with livestream hosts. Distinguish from Helping & Family Emergency: here the elder receives connection; in Helping the elder gives aid to someone presented as needing it (dual-code when both appear).
- **Insurance Protection & Payout:** The promise is insurance-style protection or a future payout: critical-illness or death coverage, policy dividends, endowment lump sums, principal returned on death. Distinguish from Safe Savings & Principal Security: this code requires an explicit coverage/payout/dividend promise; a policy disguised as a deposit via interest-and-withdrawal talk stays in Savings.
- **Helping & Family Emergency:** The scam exploits the elder's desire to help or rescue someone: fake family emergencies (relative impersonation), acquaintances borrowing under sympathetic pretexts, lodging requests, Good-Samaritan situations including 碰瓷 staged-injury extortion. Distinguish from Authority Compliance & Threat Avoidance: rescuing a relative is Helping; protecting oneself from an official threat is Compliance (dual-code when combined).
- **Authority Compliance & Threat Avoidance:** The elder is pressured to comply with a claimed authority or official requirement to avoid a threatened harm: 公检法 impersonation demanding transfers to avoid arrest or money-laundering charges, identity-misuse scares, official-sounding demands to keep benefits. Distinguish from Problem-Resolution & Recovery Services: there the elder voluntarily buys a fix; here compliance is coerced by fear.
- **Problem-Resolution & Recovery Services:** The promise is a professional service that will solve an existing problem or recoup a prior loss: loan or financial-service processing, legal representation, case reversal, recovering earlier scam losses. Distinguish from Authority Compliance & Threat Avoidance: the elder actively seeks and pays for the promised remedy rather than obeying a threat.
- **Spiritual & Superstition:** The promise is a supernatural, religious, or fortune-related benefit: karmic merit for donations (e.g. temple construction), blessings, 消灾/祈福 removal of bad luck. Distinguish from Cure & Disease Treatment: supernatural benefits with no medical claim stay here; faith-framed healing that promises to treat a disease goes to Cure.
- **N/A:** Leave this variable N/A when the post never states what was promised or which need was exploited — e.g. bare loss reports, neglect or intra-family taking with no lure described, or posts where money simply disappeared without any pitch being recounted. Do not infer a promise from the scam category alone.

- Mass-Media Advertising & Testimonial Content
- Free-Gift, Prize & Seminar Funnel
- Family & Kin Channel
- Provider, Store & Existing-Account Channel
- Bank & Insurance Sales Channel
- Street & Door-to-Door Approach
- Friend, Acquaintance & Referral Channel
- Unspecified Sales Contact
- Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation
- Offline Recruitment & Investment Solicitation
- Telemarketing & Phone Contact
- Tour & Travel-Group Funnel
- Impersonation & Pretext Contact
- E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform
- Livestream & Short-Video Commerce

**B. Commitment**

- Purchase
- Investment & Deposit
- Contract, Policy, Loan & Property Signing
- Direct Payment & Transfer
- Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration
- Joining, Membership & Recruitment
- Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction
- Treatment, Regimen & Ritual Uptake
- Relationship & Grooming Commitment
- Travel & Inspection Trip
- Access & Disclosure
- Contemplated / No Commitment
- N/A

Fill both columns for every coded post: at least one channel code in column A and at least one commitment code in column B. Column A allows up to two contact channels; column B allows up to three commitments; the combined output array allows no more than four codes. Add codes only when the route genuinely contains distinct entry points or commitments. Do not stack codes to cover uncertainty.

Note: The channel codes split on *who or what makes first contact*, not on the product. A staged lure event (free gifts, 会销 seminar, raffle) is the Seminar Funnel; a cold approach with no event is Street & Door-to-Door; a lure that is a trip is the Tour Funnel. Identity always beats medium: a disguised identity (fake police, fake son) codes to Impersonation & Pretext Contact even when it arrives by phone, and a named kin or friend beats whatever medium or product they pitched. On the commitment side, the head noun decides: goods = Purchase, money-for-returns = Investment & Deposit, a signed instrument (insurance policy, loan, contract — the 存款变保单 event) = Contract/Policy Signing, and any "forced / hidden / unauthorized" qualifier moves or dual-assigns the row into Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction.

Definitions:

**A. Contact channel**

- **Mass-Media Advertising & Testimonial Content:** One-to-many media exposure starts the route — TV ads and TV shopping, newspaper ads, online/WeChat advertisements, endorsements, and 软文 testimonial health articles. The content is broadcast, not interactive. Distinguish from Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation (two-way chat or group contact) and Livestream & Short-Video Commerce (real-time selling by a host); a relative relaying a testimonial codes to Family & Kin Channel.
- **Free-Gift, Prize & Seminar Funnel:** Contact comes through an organized lure event or freebie: free gifts or eggs, 会销 hotel seminars, health lectures and classes, free trials/checkups/therapy sessions, banquets, raffles and lottery coupons, or experience centers that funnel elders toward a sale. The staged reciprocity event is the defining feature. Distinguish from Street & Door-to-Door Approach (no event — the scammer comes to the elder) and Tour & Travel-Group Funnel (the lure is a trip, not a local event).
- **Family & Kin Channel:** The route reaches the elder through family members or relatives — as recruiters into 传销 or investment schemes, referrers, testimonial-givers, deceivers, or (in financial-abuse cases) the perpetrators themselves. The blood/marriage tie defines the code regardless of what is pitched: a relative selling insurance codes here, not to Bank & Insurance Sales Channel. Distinguish from Friend, Acquaintance & Referral Channel only by the kin relationship.
- **Provider, Store & Existing-Account Channel:** The scam operates through a commercial establishment, care or medical provider, or an existing account or purchased product — stores, clinics, hospitals, nursing homes, installers, telecom agents, prior purchases seeding upsells, hidden recurring charges, insider access to accounts. The elder is already inside a legitimate-seeming relationship when exploitation begins; no lure event and no cold approach. Routes that start from an online purchase leaking charges are dual-coded with E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform.
- **Bank & Insurance Sales Channel:** Contact through bank premises, staff, or promotions, or through insurance-company agents and salespersons — classically an elder visiting to deposit money who is redirected into a policy (存款变保单). Code by the institutional venue/personnel making contact, not the product: insurance sold by a relative or at the door codes to Family & Kin or Street & Door-to-Door. Distinguish from Provider, Store & Existing-Account Channel, which covers insider theft from an account the elder already holds.
- **Street & Door-to-Door Approach:** First contact is an unsolicited in-person approach in public or at the elder's home: street or market solicitation, bus-stop encounters, village canvassing, door-to-door pitches, stranger visits, in-person con approaches. No organized event lures the elder — the scammer physically comes to them. Distinguish from Impersonation & Pretext Contact: the stranger here uses no fake institutional or kin identity.
- **Friend, Acquaintance & Referral Channel:** Contact arrives through non-kin personal ties — friends, acquaintances, fellow believers, suitors, or unspecified referrals — whose real social trust carries the pitch, including scammers who cultivate acquaintance through repeated socializing. Distinguish from Family & Kin Channel (blood/marriage tie) and from Impersonation & Pretext Contact, where the "acquaintance" identity is fabricated rather than a real relationship being abused.
- **Unspecified Sales Contact:** Residual: the post records a sales pitch, promotion, advice, or inducement without naming any venue, medium, or relationship for the first contact. Use only when no channel information exists; any named venue, medium, or tie moves the row to a substantive code.
- **Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation:** Contact through interactive online spaces: WeChat/QQ/social-media groups, friend requests, private chats, online lecture groups, viral rumors, P2P and online lending platforms. Two-way interaction distinguishes it from Mass-Media Advertising; selling by a live host codes to Livestream & Short-Video Commerce; a self-initiated storefront transaction codes to E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform.
- **Offline Recruitment & Investment Solicitation:** Contact by recruiters or company representatives soliciting investment, project participation, or scheme membership, where no family/friend vector and no online medium is specified. If the recruiter is kin or a friend, code Family & Kin or Friend/Acquaintance instead; if recruitment happens online, code Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation.
- **Telemarketing & Phone Contact:** Contact by telephone under the caller's own, non-disguised identity: cold sales or prize calls, 10085/operator plan calls, follow-up calls after a previous purchase, repeated landline calls. Distinguish from Impersonation & Pretext Contact: a fake-police or fake-son call codes there, not here — identity disguise beats the phone medium.
- **Tour & Travel-Group Funnel:** Contact occurs inside a tourism setting: low-price or free tours (低价团), tour-group shopping stops, tour guides, tourist shops, or extra-fee coercion on paid tours. The trip itself is the funnel. Distinguish from Free-Gift, Prize & Seminar Funnel, where the lure event is local, and from Family & Kin Channel when relatives supplied the trip.
- **Impersonation & Pretext Contact:** First contact is made under a false identity or staged pretext: fake 公检法 (police/court) calls, fake kin, fake utility or company employees, WeChat impersonation, buyers posing as someone they are not, staged incident scenarios. Identity disguise takes precedence over medium — a fake-police phone call codes here, not to Telemarketing & Phone Contact. Distinguish from Friend, Acquaintance & Referral Channel, where a real relationship is exploited rather than fabricated.
- **E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform:** Contact begins with a self-initiated online storefront transaction — e-commerce listings, online phone purchases, mini-program purchases — that turns out fraudulent or seeds further exploitation. Distinguish from Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation (the elder is solicited in a chat/group rather than shopping); when the purchased product or account later leaks charges, dual-code with Provider, Store & Existing-Account Channel.
- **Livestream & Short-Video Commerce:** Contact via livestream shopping rooms or short-video commerce, often with parasocial "family" bonding between host and elderly viewers. The real-time performative selling and host relationship separate it from static Online Groups/Chat and from broadcast Mass-Media ads.

**B. Commitment**

- **Purchase:** The elder is moved to buy goods or product-like services — one-off, repeated, or bulk — including overpriced, counterfeit, or medically useless products (保健品, devices, medicines bought as boxed products, jewelry, collectibles, water purifiers, memberships-as-cards). Money-for-goods distinguishes it from Direct Payment & Transfer (money with nothing bought) and Investment & Deposit (money placed for returns); insurance "purchases" code to Contract, Policy, Loan & Property Signing, and forced or coerced purchases code to Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction.
- **Investment & Deposit:** The elder commits money expecting returns: investments, deposits into schemes, platforms, or apps, shares, equity agreements, tokens, or fund placements — usually escalating and repeated. Financial instruments code here even when the label says "purchase" (shares, equity, tokens); physical collectibles stay in Purchase, and money handed over with no return story is Direct Payment & Transfer.
- **Contract, Policy, Loan & Property Signing:** The elder signs a binding instrument: insurance policies (the 存款变保单 pattern), service contracts and plan signups, loan or mortgage documents, powers of attorney, property transfers, or business setups. The harm is locked in by a signed arrangement rather than a money handover. Distinguish from Investment & Deposit: a bank fixed-term deposit without a policy codes there.
- **Direct Payment & Transfer:** The elder hands over money as such — bank transfers, cash handoffs, fees, top-ups, upfront treatment payments, or lending to a person — without receiving goods or an investment vehicle. No goods (Purchase) and no return narrative (Investment & Deposit); payments extracted by coercion or without authorization code to Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction. The elder lending money out codes here; the elder signing as borrower codes to Contract/Loan Signing.
- **Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration:** The elder is moved into continued interaction short of paying or joining: attending lectures, meetings, seminars, or staged sales shows, downloading apps, visiting websites or clinics, performing tasks, or migrating to a private channel such as a WeChat group. Often the mid-funnel step. Distinguish from Joining, Membership & Recruitment: attending and interacting, without organizational affiliation.
- **Joining, Membership & Recruitment:** The elder affiliates with a scheme or organization: joining 传销/pyramid schemes, paying memberships, becoming a downline recruiter, enrolling, or entering a facility or program. Affiliative commitment distinguishes it from Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration; recruiting others (downlines) always codes here.
- **Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction:** Money or property is taken without a voluntary commitment: theft by trusted persons, unauthorized deductions and pension transfers, hidden recurring or data charges, forced/coerced purchases and fees, forced enrollment, blackmail, staged compensation demands. Absence of genuine consent separates it from every voluntary-commitment code; rows mixing voluntary and imposed elements are dual-coded (e.g. policy purchase + automatic deductions).
- **Treatment, Regimen & Ritual Uptake:** The elder undergoes a health treatment, procedure, regimen, or superstition ritual — therapy courses, unproven drug regimens, restrictive diets, surgeries, clinic visits, luck-removal rituals. The commitment is the elder's body and behavior, not only money. Head-noun rule: buying medicine as a boxed product stays in Purchase; a course, regimen, or session undergone codes here.
- **Relationship & Grooming Commitment:** The elder is drawn into an emotional, romantic, or parasocial bond — private WeChat grooming, courtship, marriage, staged intimacy — as the commitment the scam runs on. The bond itself is the secured commitment; downstream money events are dual-coded. Distinguish from Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration, which is transactional interaction without emotional bonding.
- **Travel & Inspection Trip:** The elder commits to traveling — "project" site inspections, overseas trips, or travel bundled into recruitment — as part of the scam's escalation. Distinguish from Tour & Travel-Group Funnel on the channel axis: there tourism is how contact is made; here the trip is a demanded commitment inside the route.
- **Access & Disclosure:** The commitment is granting entry or exposing information: verifying accounts, letting scammers into the home, or having personal/SIM data captured through a compromised product. The extracted good is access or data, not money — covert data capture stays here rather than in Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction (money/property outcome).
- **Contemplated / No Commitment:** The route stops before any commitment: the elder was contacted or is merely considering the pitch, typically in seeking-verification posts written pre-commitment, or scam calls reaching the household with no action taken. Distinguished from every other commitment code by the absence of any completed action; the contact channel is still coded normally in column A.
- **N/A:** Leave both columns N/A when the post contains no contact-to-commitment episode at all — general discussion, policy commentary, or PSA posts with no scammer reaching a specific elder (e.g. a debate thread on whether to help a fallen elder given 碰瓷 risk). If any concrete route is narrated, however thin, code it instead of N/A.

## 6. Asset Extracted (single or multi-select (up to two))

- Health
- Property

Code up to two distinct extracted assets. Use a second code only when the text supports a separate asset type (e.g. Money + Health in 保健品 cases, Money + Property in theft cases). Never code more than two.

Note: The four "non-cash" codes are separated by what actually leaves the elder. Funds already paid out = Money; a specific tangible thing or real-asset interest handed over = Property; a still-binding legal commitment to pay in the future = Contract or Debt Obligation; personal information that enables further fraud = Data. Bodily harm from the scheme is Health, and effort/time/care sunk into it is Labor — neither is a substitute for Money when payments also occurred; code both.

Definitions:

- **Money:** Direct financial extraction — cash, bank transfers, deposits, purchase payments for fake or overpriced products, investment/传销 buy-ins, insurance premiums, and unauthorized fees or phone charges (吸费, 乱扣费). This is the dominant asset and covers funds that have already left the elder's hands, whatever their form. Distinguish from Contract or Debt Obligation: money already paid is Money; a liability for future payments is the contract/debt code. Distinguish from Data: bank INFORMATION is Data; funds later moved with it are Money.

- **Health:** The elder's bodily health harmed or put at risk as a cost of the scam — ingesting fake cures or 三无 products, undergoing pseudoscientific 理疗, or forgoing real medical treatment on the scammer's advice. This may be coded alongside Money, which covers the payment while Health covers the physical-harm dimension. Distinguish from Labor: Health is what the scheme does to the victim's body, not effort the victim expends.

- **Property:** Physical goods, valuables, or real assets taken from or ceded by the elder — jewelry, phones, cash-adjacent valuables stolen in trust-building burglaries, pets, or housing/real-estate interests signed away. Distinguish from Money: Property is a non-fungible tangible asset (a jade bracelet handed over is Property; the cash paid for one is Money).

- **Contract or Debt Obligation:** A binding financial commitment imposed on the elder through deception — unwanted 存款变保单-style policies, bundled multi-year telecom plans, subscriptions, or loan/debt liabilities taken out in the elder's name. The extracted harm is the ongoing obligation itself, even if little cash has left yet. Distinguish from Property: no tangible asset changes hands; what is extracted is a legal commitment.

- **Labor:** The elder's (or family's) time, effort, care work, or emotional labor extracted by the scheme — years of sustained 传销 participation, caregiving and affective investment drained by romance/marriage fraud or exploitative kin. Distinguish from Money: Labor is expended effort and time, not funds; when both are drained, code both.

- **Data:** Personal information extracted from the elder — identity details, phone numbers/WeChat contacts harvested in marketing schemes, or bank/account information usable for further fraud. Data is the informational enabler, not the funds themselves; when identity data is used to saddle the elder with a fraudulent loan, code the data as Data and the liability as Contract or Debt Obligation.

- **N/A:** Leave N/A when the scam episode never reaches (or even aims at) extraction of any identifiable asset from an elder — e.g. general discussion threads (碰瓷/扶不扶 debates), harassment or nuisance calls with no loss described, and complaint posts where nothing was actually taken or committed. Do not force a code onto posts that describe no extracted or targeted value.

- Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals
- Deceptive Pricing & Extreme Markup
- Non-Delivery, Withdrawal Failure & Vanishing
- Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods
- Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics
- Hidden Terms & Covert Charges
- Unrealistic Financial Promise
- Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands
- False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials
- Fabricated Social Proof & Staged Demonstrations
- Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine
- Free-Gift, Prize & Low-Price Bait
- Isolation, Secrecy & Warning Resistance
- Financial Product Bait-and-Switch
- Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination
- Impersonation & False Identity
- Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access
- Unsolicited or Stranger Approach
- N/A

Code every warning sign the post itself identifies, one code per distinct sign, up to five. One code fits a simple episode; reserve four or five for genuinely dense episodes. Do not infer red flags from the scam category alone.

Note: Several code pairs sit close together and must be split on a specific question. Ask what is wrong with the item: its price relative to worth (Deceptive Pricing & Extreme Markup), its identity — provably fake (Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods), or merely that nothing about it can be verified (Unverifiable Legitimacy). Ask how the money moves: demanded openly and repeatedly (Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands), taken or concealed covertly (Hidden Terms & Covert Charges), or via a product mislabeled at the point of sale (Financial Product Bait-and-Switch). Ask how compliance is produced: fear and force (Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals), positive affect and belief change (Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination), or small material freebies (Free-Gift, Prize & Low-Price Bait). Ask whose credibility is faked: an institution or document (False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials), a person the scammer pretends to be (Impersonation & False Identity), or peers and outcomes (Fabricated Social Proof & Staged Demonstrations) — versus a real trusted relationship being abused (Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access).

Definitions:

- **Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims:** A product or treatment is claimed, without evidence, to cure, prevent, or treat disease — cure-all (包治百病) and no-side-effect claims, pseudoscientific mechanisms (quantum/nanotech, 9000V therapy, 祖传秘方), and temporary effects passed off as cures. This is the signature red flag of 保健品/会销 health-product scams. Distinguish from Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine: this code captures the false benefit claim itself, not practices that actively endanger health.

- **Elder-Vulnerability Targeting:** The scam deliberately selects elders or exploits an elder-specific incapacity — illiteracy, cognitive impairment, inability to read contracts, unclear consent, elders-only venues, pension (养老) pretexts, or elder-only surcharges. Distinguish from Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access: this codes WHO is targeted and which incapacity is exploited, not which real relationship is abused.

- **Unverifiable Legitimacy:** The seller, product, or story fails basic verification: unknown or obscure brands, no regulatory approval or licence, no manufacturer details or receipts, shell or brand-new companies, vague or unverifiable assets and claims, inconsistent identifying details. The test is absence — "cannot confirm it is real." Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods (the item is proven fake) and from False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials (credentials are affirmatively fabricated rather than simply absent).

- **Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals:** Compliance is forced through urgency, repetition, aggression, or manufactured fear — high-pressure sales and upselling, forced purchases, threats (abandonment, prosecution, blackmail), harassment, health fearmongering, and fabricated emergencies. Distinguish from Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination: this code works through aversive force and fear, not positive affect.

- **Deceptive Pricing & Extreme Markup:** Price-value deception: inflated or fake "original" prices, fake discounts, extreme markups on low-value goods, exaggerated value or rarity, tourist-venue markups, and elder-specific surcharges. Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods: this codes what the item COSTS relative to its worth, not what the item IS; the two frequently co-occur and are then co-assigned.

- **Non-Delivery, Withdrawal Failure & Vanishing:** After payment, the promised goods, services, payouts, or withdrawals never materialize — refund refusals, frozen accounts, suspended withdrawals, endless delays and excuses, unreachable sellers, companies that disappear or abscond. Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods (a fake thing DOES arrive) and from Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands (demands for more money rather than failure to deliver).

- **Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods:** The thing sold or delivered is not what it purports to be: counterfeits, refurbished-as-new devices, worthless or nonfunctional goods, product substitutions, brand confusion, and sham financial instruments (fake shares, accounts, cards). Distinguish from Unverifiable Legitimacy: here the item is affirmatively fake or misrepresented, not merely impossible to verify.

- **Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics:** Structural red flags of unsustainable schemes: entry fees and pay-to-join, downline/recruitment hierarchies (传销) and recruitment-funded returns, inventory stockpiling, new money paying earlier investors, and unlicensed mass fundraising (非法集资), including 资本运作-style jargon. Distinguish from Unrealistic Financial Promise: this codes the scheme's funding architecture, not the payoff pitched to each victim.

- **Hidden Terms & Covert Charges:** Material facts or money are taken or withheld covertly: hidden surrender fees, lock-ins and penalties, legal-document traps and over-broad authorizations, unauthorized or automatic deductions, preinstalled fee software and trojans, unexplained charges, evidence destruction. Distinguish from Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands (payments demanded openly) and from Financial Product Bait-and-Switch (the product's identity is misrepresented at sale).

- **Unrealistic Financial Promise:** The scammer offers returns, payouts, buybacks, subsidies, or benefits that are implausibly favorable or contractually empty — guaranteed high interest, double-digit monthly returns, promised buybacks, verbal promises never honored. Promises of money or material benefit only; health-cure promises go to Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims. Distinguish from Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics: this codes the pitch content, not the scheme structure behind it.

- **Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands:** The payment pattern itself is the red flag: repeated or escalating fees, deposits, and transfers; pay-more demands; full prepayment; debt-funded participation; excessive or bulk buying; and irregular channels such as cash couriers or off-platform payment. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges: these payments are openly demanded, not covertly taken.

- **False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials:** Borrowed or fabricated backing legitimizes the scam: fake government affiliation, projects, policies, or notices; official, celebrity, or divine endorsements; insider-access claims; forged certificates, stamps, policies, and company records. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: this codes fabricated BACKING and papers, not posing as a person or organization in interaction.

- **Fabricated Social Proof & Staged Demonstrations:** Manufactured peer or outcome evidence: paid shills (托), actors posing as patients, staged testimonials and prize reactions, advertorials disguised as independent content, and anecdotal recovery or income stories used as proof. Distinguish from False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials: the faked proof here comes from peers, patients, or outcomes, not from institutions or officials.

- **Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine:** The scam actively endangers the elder's health: inducing them to stop medication or refuse hospitals, unsafe procedures and dosages, harmful regimens, undeclared drugs, unsafe devices, and caregiver medical neglect or abuse. Distinguish from Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims: this codes physical endangerment, not the false benefit claim.

- **Free-Gift, Prize & Low-Price Bait:** Small up-front material lures — free gifts, checkups (免费体检), tours, trials, prize wins, below-cost prices, or small initial investment profits — hook the elder into much larger extraction. The lure is actually delivered up front. Distinguish from Unrealistic Financial Promise: that code covers large FUTURE benefits merely promised, not small freebies handed over now.

- **Isolation, Secrecy & Warning Resistance:** The elder is cut off from corrective voices: secrecy demands, family isolation, movement to private channels, elderly-only venues, concealment of losses from family, and disregard of family, bank, or police warnings. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges: here the victim is hidden from others; there, facts are hidden from the victim.

- **Financial Product Bait-and-Switch:** A financial product is sold as something it is not — the classic 存款变保单 pattern: insurance disguised as a bank deposit, savings product, or investment, with terms misrepresented at sale and verbal promises contradicting the written contract. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges (terms merely buried in fine print) and from Unrealistic Financial Promise (implausible returns without product mislabeling).

- **Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination:** Affection, belonging, or ideology is cultivated to disable skepticism: emotional manipulation and storytelling, brainwashing (洗脑), motivational and success-culture indoctrination, fictive kinship, and overfriendly trust-building toward isolated elders. Distinguish from Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals: this works through positive affect and belief change, not fear or force.

- **Impersonation & False Identity:** The scammer pretends to be someone or something they are not: a family member, a utility/telecom/bank employee, a lawyer or military official, or a fabricated persona — including fear pretexts delivered under that guise. Distinguish from Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access: here the relationship is fake; there, a real one is abused.

- **Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access:** A genuinely trusted party — a child or relative, caregiver, bank insider, or befriended acquaintance — abuses real access to the elder or their assets: thefts by children, caregiver abuse, insider transfers, copied keys after befriending, recruitment through one's own family. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: the relationship or access here is real, not faked.

- **Unsolicited or Stranger Approach:** The contact or transaction is initiated out of the blue by an unknown party: stranger solicitation, unsolicited promotions, calls, or official-sounding notices, services activated without request, dealings with complete strangers. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: this codes the UNINVITED nature of the contact itself, not who the contact pretends to be.

- **N/A:** Use only when the post identifies no warning sign at all — e.g., a bare loss report or general discussion that describes no deceptive claim, manipulative tactic, structural irregularity, or exploitation cue. N/A is an exception, not a fallback.

Code all distinct responses that apply, up to three. Do not force an extra code merely to fill the cap. Code each distinct actor-action pair: "family reported, victim believed" takes one code per actor.

Note: Several pairs sit close together and must be split by actor, target, or channel. Report To Authorities & Legal Action vs. Seek Remedy From Seller: Report escalates to a third-party institution (police, 12315, courts, regulators, platforms), Remedy negotiates with the scam counterparty itself — a post doing both gets both. Warn Others & Public Exposure addresses the public; Dissuade & Confront Elder addresses one's own elder. Question Doubt & Verify checks one's own transaction; Investigate & Gather Evidence probes the scheme or perpetrator. Accept Comply is initial compliance; Continue Persist In Belief is persistence after pushback; Promote Advocate Scheme adds outward recruitment. Reject Refuse never engaged; Stop Disengage breaks off engagement already underway.

Definitions:

- **Report To Authorities & Legal Action:** The victim, family, or victims collectively escalate to formal institutions: reporting to police or the 12315 hotline, complaining to consumer-protection bodies or regulators, reporting accounts to platforms, suing the seller, or pursuing courts, prosecutors, 维权, or divorce/compensation claims. Distinguish from Seek Remedy From Seller: Report involves third-party enforcement institutions, not direct negotiation with the counterparty; "reported to police and exposed online" labels are coded here AND under Warn Others & Public Exposure.

- **Seek Remedy From Seller:** The elder or their family pursues remediation directly from the seller, provider, or scheme counterparty: demanding refunds, returns, or cancellations, requesting withdrawals from investment schemes, submitting insurance claims or challenging denials, disputing charges, confronting salespeople, or contacting customer service. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action (escalation to police/regulators/courts) and from Stop Disengage: Remedy tries to claw money back, Stop merely ceases further engagement.

- **Warn Others & Public Exposure:** Alerting people beyond the household: publicly exposing the scheme, company, streamer, or suspect; warning other consumers, investors, villagers, or viewers; enlisting media; or official public-education warnings relayed in news/PSA posts. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action: the audience here is the PUBLIC, not an enforcement institution. Mirror image of Promote Advocate Scheme.

- **Seek Help & Advice:** Turning to the Tieba community, peers, or other informal sources for advice, identification, guidance, emotional support, or practical help — including the elder disclosing her own plight. This is the signature Tieba response: the post itself is often the help-seeking act by the victim or their adult child. Distinguish from Question Doubt & Verify: asking "what should I do?" versus asking "is this real / what is it worth?"; help sought from police or regulators belongs under Report To Authorities & Legal Action.

- **Question Doubt & Verify:** The suspicious elder or (more often) a family member questions legitimacy and checks the specific transaction, product, or policy: seeking appraisal or valuation, verifying with the genuine institution (bank, carrier, water company), reviewing contracts and bills, voicing skeptical critique of the claims, or belatedly realizing the deception. Distinguish from Investigate & Gather Evidence: Verify checks one's OWN purchase/policy; Investigate probes the scheme, company, or perpetrator itself.

- **Dissuade & Confront Elder:** Family members try to change the elder's behavior through persuasion, argument, confrontation, in-family warnings, restraint, or benevolent counter-trickery — the family-intervention response distinctive of the Tieba caregiver vantage point. Distinguish from Reject Refuse (refusing a demand aimed at oneself) and from Take Control & Secure Finances Care: dissuasion works through communication, whereas Secure imposes structural control over money, care, or accounts.

- **Accept Comply:** The elder (or occasionally the household) went along with the scam at least once: believed the pitch, purchased, invested, paid fees, handed over cash or valuables, or underwent the promoted treatment. In sequential narratives ("paid, then reported") this captures the initial compliance phase before any later remedial action. Distinguish from Continue Persist In Belief, which requires persisting AFTER red flags surfaced or family objected.

- **Investigate & Gather Evidence:** Active probing of the scheme, company, or perpetrator beyond checking one's own transaction: researching how the scam works, going undercover or posing as an insider, monitoring the scammer's group, analyzing the business model, or collecting and preserving evidence. Usually the adult child or a whistleblowing poster. Distinguish from Question Doubt & Verify (checking one's own purchase) and from Report To Authorities & Legal Action: investigation here is self-directed detective work, not handing the matter to institutions.

- **Promote Advocate Scheme:** The poster or the elder actively promotes, endorses, or recruits for the scam product or scheme — evangelism rather than resistance. Covers deeply-converted victims who become advocates and posts written by promoters themselves. Distinguish from Continue Persist In Belief: promotion adds outward recruitment or advertising on top of private belief.

- **Take Control & Secure Finances Care:** Family members impose protective structural control over the elder's money, accounts, care, or living situation: taking over finances, freezing billing, taking over caregiving, monitoring care, repaying scam-induced debts, or arranging medical/psychological help. Distinguish from Dissuade & Confront Elder: Secure changes the elder's ENVIRONMENT rather than their mind; distinguish from Stop Disengage, which ends a specific payment stream rather than establishing ongoing control.

- **Reject Refuse:** The target — the elder, or more often a family member being recruited through the elder — refuses the offer, resists or evades recruitment (typically into 传销), declines to hand over money, or flees the scheme's control. Distinguish from Dissuade & Confront Elder: Reject is refusing a demand made of ONESELF; distinguish from Stop Disengage, which requires prior engagement that is then broken off.

- **Continue Persist In Belief:** The elder keeps believing in or engaging with the scam despite family opposition, warnings, or emerging evidence — or a previously skeptical family member wavers back toward belief. Distinguish from Accept Comply (one-time or initial compliance): Continue requires persistence AGAINST pushback or red flags. Private persistence only; recruiting others is Promote Advocate Scheme.

- **Stop Disengage:** Breaking off an engagement already underway: halting further payments or treatment, canceling an order, cutting off funding, or pulling invested money out. Actor may be the elder or the family cutting off the money flow. Distinguish from Reject Refuse (never engaged) and from Seek Remedy From Seller: Stop is unilateral cessation, Remedy asks the counterparty to give money back.

- **Collective Victim Action:** Victims or their families act AS A GROUP: forming victim groups, organizing fellow victims, seeking witnesses, mass-submitting case materials, or pursuing recovery through collective protest — the 维权 repertoire of large Chinese investment/fundraising collapses. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action and Seek Remedy From Seller by its organized, collective character; a label naming both an individual channel and collective organizing takes both codes.

- **Distress & Regret:** Emotional-state responses without (or overshadowing) instrumental action: severe emotional distress, post-purchase regret, or scam-induced family strain intense enough to threaten relationships. Distress that merely accompanies an action (e.g., a tearful refund demand) is coded under the action, not here.

- **Other Respond:** Residual for responses fitting no victim-, family-, or community-side code — for example, a perpetrator-side response such as a self-identified scammer's public confession claiming to have repented and abandoned fraud. Do not force such posts into Stop Disengage, whose actor is a victim or family member.

- **N/A:** Leave this variable N/A when the post narrates no response action by the elder, the family, or fellow victims — for example, bare news reposts or link shares and pure scheme descriptions in which no one's reaction to the scam is recorded. Use N/A rather than inferring a response the text does not state.

Code every distinct protective, investigative, remedial, or accountability action that appears in the episode, up to four. Do not pad the list with marginal actions.

Note: Several codes sit close together and split on actor or channel, not on the underlying act. Publicizing splits three ways: Warn alerts others to a specific scam/scammer, Fraud Education teaches generalizable anti-fraud knowledge (科普/反诈宣传), and Public Appeal & Advocacy asks the public for help or justice on behalf of an existing victim. Fact-finding splits on who does it: Check is the poster's or family's own verification, Consult outsources judgment to the online community, and Media Investigation is the same work done by journalists. Formal reporting splits on channel: Report to Police is the criminal channel; Regulatory & Platform Complaint is the administrative/commercial channel (12315/315, 工商局, regulators, platforms); Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention targets the offending company or the elder's own bank/insurer/carrier directly. Family-side action splits on mechanism: Coordinate is interpersonal persuasion and supervision, Secure Funds & Devices is structural control of money or devices, and Care & Health Protection remediates harm to the elder's body and wellbeing.

Definitions:

- **Warn:** Making the scam publicly known to protect others — online or public exposure (曝光), forum warnings, public complaints, and warnings issued through media, police, lawyers, or consumer associations. Distinguish from Fraud Education: Warn flags a particular scam or scammer, Education teaches general anti-fraud practice. Distinguish from Public Appeal & Advocacy: Warn protects future victims, Appeal seeks help or justice for an existing one.
- **Check:** Verifying facts, legitimacy, identities, products, prices, bills, or documents to establish whether a scam occurred and how — online research, appraisals (incl. 鉴定/UV inspection), regulatory-database checks, contract/bill/policy review, price analysis, or directly probing the suspect party. Distinguish from Consult: Check is fact-finding the poster or family performs themselves; asking peers for judgment is Consult (a post can carry both). Media doing the same work is Media Investigation.
- **Coordinate:** Family members and relatives intervening directly with the elder — confronting, counter-persuading, questioning, warning, advising, supervising, or monitoring them to interrupt or prevent the scam. Distinguish from Secure Funds & Devices: Coordinate is persuasion and oversight of the person; restricting their money or devices is Secure Funds & Devices. Distinguish from Care & Health Protection: Coordinate counters the scam itself, Care remediates harm already done.
- **Consult:** Turning to the online community — this Tieba or other forums/groups — for advice, judgment, sense-making, or help with what to do next, including open discussion of the poster's own case. Distinguish from Warn: discussion seeks input for one's own case, Warn broadcasts to protect others. Professional legal advice is Legal, not Consult.
- **Report to Police:** Engaging the police/criminal-justice apparatus: filing 报警/报案, calling or contacting police, submissions to police-run anti-fraud channels, police investigations, and police-coordinated stings. Distinguish from Regulatory & Platform Complaint: police are the criminal channel; 12315/315, regulators, and platforms are the administrative channel — compound episodes often carry both. Frontline bank staff calling police also carries Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention.
- **Regulatory & Platform Complaint:** Filing formal complaints or reports through China's non-police accountability channels: consumer hotlines (12315/315), 工商局 and sector regulators, and e-commerce/app platform reporting and dispute mechanisms; bare "complained/投诉" with no stated channel defaults here. Distinguish from Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention: complaints here go to third-party oversight bodies or platform mechanisms, not to the offending company or one's own bank/insurer/carrier. Distinguish from Warn: these are formal filings, not public airing.
- **Document:** Collecting, recording, or preserving evidence of the scam — records, recordings, photos, videos, chat logs, bills, and financial documents — whether or not it is later submitted anywhere. Distinguish from Check: Document preserves proof for later use, Check establishes what is true. When evidence is submitted to police or a platform, also code the receiving channel.
- **Media Investigation:** Investigative and verification work performed by journalists or media outlets as third-party actors: media investigations, undercover reporting, reporter verification, fact-checks, and news reports documenting the scam case. Distinguish from Check: same epistemic work, but the actor is professional media rather than the poster or family. Distinguish from Warn: this code covers the investigative process; the publicizing output is Warn (many episodes carry both).
- **Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention:** Directly engaging the involved company or the elder's own service institution — bank, insurer, telecom carrier, seller's customer service — to question, complain to, or seek remedy from it, plus protective interventions initiated by frontline institution staff themselves (e.g., bank staff calling police). Distinguish from Regulatory & Platform Complaint: the counterparty here is the company or institution itself, not an oversight body. Explicit refund/return segments go to Recover.
- **Legal:** Engaging the formal legal system or legal professionals: lawyer consultations, hiring lawyers, lawsuits, court actions and prosecutions, legal complaints, legal-aid engagement, and legal planning. Distinguish from Report to Police: civil/court and lawyer-mediated routes versus criminal reporting. The public campaigning side of 维权 is Public Appeal & Advocacy, not Legal.
- **Secure Funds & Devices:** Restricting the elder's exposure through financial or technical controls: cutting off or separating finances, withholding funds, payment controls, or blocking devices/network access (e.g., router blocking). Distinguish from Coordinate: these are structural restrictions on money and devices, not persuasion or supervision. Distinguish from Recover: Secure prevents further loss, Recover claws back money already lost.
- **Public Appeal & Advocacy:** Appealing to the public, media, or society for help, attention, or justice on behalf of a victim, and organized victim-advocacy/rights-defense (维权) activity, including anti-MLM (反传销) rescue interventions. Distinguish from Warn: an appeal seeks help or justice for an existing victim, Warn alerts potential future victims. Distinguish from Legal: 维权 campaigning is extra-judicial pressure; filed lawsuits are Legal.
- **Fraud Education:** General anti-fraud pedagogy (科普/反诈宣传): teaching elders, families, investors, or the public how scams work and what protective practices to adopt, beyond warning about one specific scam — case-based education, investor education, police-delivered education, family education of the elder, and protective-practice advice. Distinguish from Warn: Education transfers generalizable knowledge, Warn flags a particular scam. Distinguish from Coordinate: teaching the elder to self-protect is Education; confrontation or supervision that directly interrupts a scam is Coordinate.
- **Recover:** Attempting to get money or goods back after the scam: refund requests and negotiations (incl. media-brokered), product returns, and recovery through legal aid or advocacy. Distinguish from Legal: Recover targets restitution specifically; lawsuits pursued for accountability are Legal. Distinguish from Secure Funds & Devices: recovery is after-loss, controls are before or during.
- **Care & Health Protection:** Protective work aimed at the elder's body and wellbeing rather than their money: calling an ambulance, arranging emergency or ongoing medical care after 保健品/therapy scams, mental-health support, and emotional or practical family support in the aftermath. Distinguish from Coordinate: Care remediates harm the elder has already suffered — especially health harm — while Coordinate intervenes against the scam itself. Verifying medical claims is Check, not Care.
- **N/A:** No protective, investigative, remedial, or accountability action by anyone — poster, family, community, media, or institution — is narrated or planned in the episode: e.g., a scam story or scammer's own account told with no response taken, or a post that only describes the scheme. Code N/A rather than forcing an action, but note that merely posting to ask the forum for judgment is itself Consult, so pure help-seeking posts are not N/A.

## 10. One-Off vs. Repeated (single-select)

- One-Off
- Repeated
- N/A

Coding instruction: Select exactly one code. This is a binary determination about the number of extraction events over time — do not use compounds. Note that unlike the Reddit codebook there is no "unspecified" value here: when the post describes a scam episode, commit to One-Off or Repeated.

Note: The criterion is the count of extraction events, not the duration of a single transaction. A lengthy one-time negotiation (e.g., an hours-long sales pitch ending in one purchase) is still One-Off; a second purchase, a top-up, or any recurring charge flips it to Repeated.

Definitions:

- **One-Off:** The scam involved a single extraction event — one purchase, one payment, one transfer, or one signed contract — with no recurrence described. Typical cases: a single overpriced purchase (tour-stop jewelry, street-vendor goods, TV-ad orders), one 存款变保单 insurance signing, one phone-call transfer. Distinguish from Repeated: if the post mentions even a second extraction event or an ongoing charging mechanism, code Repeated instead.

- **Repeated:** The victim was extracted from multiple times or on an ongoing basis — repeated purchases, recurring charges, escalating deposits, or sustained involvement in a scheme. Typical cases: 保健品 seminar cycles, 传销 participation, repeated investment top-ups, recurring unauthorized billing (吸费). Requires at least two distinct extraction events or a continuing extraction mechanism (subscription, ongoing scheme). Distinguish from One-Off: continuity or recurrence of extraction is the deciding feature, not how long a single transaction took.

- **N/A:** Leave N/A when the post contains no scam episode with an identifiable extraction (or attempted extraction) whose recurrence could be judged — e.g., abstract discussion threads (碰瓷/扶不扶 debates), general PSA content with no concrete victim episode. Do not use N/A to dodge a hard binary call on an actual incident.

- Police Enforcement Action
- Police Investigation / Response
- Police Declined / No Effective Action
- Judicial Process
- Market & Consumer Regulators
- Government & Community Authorities
- Bank Intervention
- Insurer Response
- Seller / Company Response
- Telecom Carrier Response
- Platform Moderation
- Media Exposure & Investigation
- Medical & Care Institutions
- Civil Society & Legal Aid
- N/A

Code every third-party actor the episode names, up to two. Add a second code only when two distinct actors intervene; never code more than two. Do not force a code when no third-party actor appears.

Note: The three police codes form an outcome ladder for a single actor — coercive result (Police Enforcement Action), engagement without a stated result (Police Investigation / Response), explicit refusal or no effect (Police Declined / No Effective Action). Pick exactly one police rung per episode. Similarly, keep the counterparty business (Seller / Company Response) apart from the marketplace hosting it (Platform Moderation) and from the billing carrier (Telecom Carrier Response), and keep the deposit bank (Bank Intervention) apart from the insurer whose policy is disputed (Insurer Response) — bancassurance 存款变保单 compounds take both.

Definitions:

- **Police Enforcement Action:** Police took a concrete coercive or remedial step against the scammers — arrest or detention of suspects or ringleaders, recovery of funds, or coercive measures such as travel restrictions on executives. Distinguish from Police Investigation / Response: this code requires a stated arrest, recovery, or other coercive outcome, not mere police engagement.

- **Police Investigation / Response:** Police were contacted, attended the scene, received complaints, or opened or conducted an investigation, with no stated arrest, recovery, or refusal — the middle tier of the police outcome ladder. Includes generic "报警/police intervened" mentions and police appearing alongside another actor without a failure qualifier.

- **Police Declined / No Effective Action:** Police were approached but declined to act, referred the case elsewhere, cited insufficient evidence, or their involvement produced no remedy. Distinguish from Police Investigation / Response: an explicit negative outcome ("不管", refused, no effective action) must be stated, not merely no result yet.

- **Judicial Process:** Courts, prosecutors (检察院), arbitration bodies, or lawyers were engaged in the case, whatever the outcome — conviction and imprisonment as much as a dismissed claim. Distinguish from Civil Society & Legal Aid, which covers non-state legal-aid services and volunteers rather than the formal legal system.

- **Market & Consumer Regulators:** Consumer-protection and market-supervision bodies — the 12315 hotline, 工商/market regulator, drug regulator, consumer associations, industry associations — were engaged, with outcomes ranging from investigation to refusal or an unreachable hotline. Distinguish from Government & Community Authorities: this code needs a named specialized watchdog with a consumer-protection mandate, not generic 政府.

- **Government & Community Authorities:** Generic government bodies or grassroots governance organs — ministries, local authorities, 村委会, 乡镇/社区 staff, or unnamed "authorities" — were involved, notified, or urged to act. Appeals demanding intervention count even when no action has yet been delivered. Distinguish from the police codes, Market & Consumer Regulators, and Judicial Process, which take named specialized bodies.

- **Bank Intervention:** A bank acted, or refused to act, on the victim's accounts or funds — blocking a withdrawal or transfer, returning principal, processing a cancellation, paying token compensation, or refusing resolution. Distinguish from Insurer Response: the bank is the deposit/payment institution; when a 存款变保单 label names both bank and insurer, code both.

- **Insurer Response:** An insurance company responded to the dispute over a mis-sold policy or claim — denying a claim, refusing a full refund or withdrawal, offering a premium refund, failing to resolve, or responding with no stated outcome. Distinguish from Bank Intervention: the actor here is the insurer handling the policy at issue.

- **Seller / Company Response:** The selling business itself (or an implicated/impersonated company) responded to the complaint — refunding, refusing a refund or return, denying allegations or affiliation, deflecting through customer service, or disappearing outright. Distinguish from Platform Moderation: this is the accused counterparty, not the marketplace hosting it.

- **Telecom Carrier Response:** A mobile/telecom carrier or its customer service handled the dispute over charges or services — confirming deductions, refusing service, leaving a complaint pending, or declaring itself unable to resolve. Distinguish from Seller / Company Response: the actor is the operator whose billing or line service is at issue.

- **Platform Moderation:** An e-commerce or content platform acted, or was urged to act, on scam content or transactions it hosts — customer-service intervention, limiting recommendations, official denials, or user appeals to ban a streamer. "Urged to ban" counts as platform-directed intervention even though the action is only demanded. Distinguish from Seller / Company Response: the platform is the intermediary, not the seller.

- **Media Exposure & Investigation:** News media (newspapers, TV stations) investigated, exposed, or warned about the scam, sometimes jointly with regulators or witnesses. A poster's appeal to media that produced no media action does not qualify. Distinguish from Market & Consumer Regulators: journalism, not a state watchdog — when both act, code both.

- **Medical & Care Institutions:** Hospitals, ambulance services, or nursing-home departments intervened in the aftermath — diagnosing or treating scam-related health harm, remedying botched services, or being notified about the incident. Distinct from all remedy-seeking codes: the intervention targets health or care harm rather than money.

- **Civil Society & Legal Aid:** Non-state helpers — 反传销 (anti-MLM) volunteer rescuers or legal-aid services — stepped in, typically where formal institutions stalled. Distinguish from Judicial Process: these are non-state, non-commercial actors, not courts or retained lawyers.

- **N/A:** Leave this variable N/A when no third-party institution, business, or organized actor appears in the episode — the poster describes, warns about, or asks about the scam without any bank, police, regulator, company, platform, media outlet, or care institution having been contacted or having acted. Do not force a code onto a purely family-internal or advice-seeking narrative.

## 12. Emotional Frame (one or two dominant frames; never more than two)

- Anger & Outrage
- Protective Concern
- Warning & Caution
- Frustration & Exasperation
- Helplessness & Desperation
- Doubt & Suspicion
- Fear & Alarm
- Promotional Appeal
- Grief & Hurt
- Urgency
- Justice & Injustice
- Betrayal & Distrust
- Regret & Moral Reflection
- Caregiver Strain & Distress
- Ridicule & Sarcasm
- Empathy & Sympathy
- Relief & Vindication
- Help-Seeking

Code the one or two dominant affective stances of the post; never code more than two. Every post gets at least one frame — this variable is never left empty.

Note: Several codes are deliberately close siblings and must be split by appraisal, not by intensity. Anger & Outrage is moral heat at a wrongdoer; Frustration & Exasperation is worn-down irritation at a blocked goal (a parent who won't listen, a hotline that stonewalls); Justice & Injustice is the explicit claim of being wronged and the demand for redress — the three routinely co-occur but are distinct. Protective Concern is inward-facing worry for one's own elder; Warning & Caution is outward-facing alerting of the community; Fear & Alarm is acute dread rather than diffuse worry. Doubt & Suspicion is pre-verdict uncertainty ("is this a scam?"); Betrayal & Distrust is the post-verdict rupture of trust. Helplessness & Desperation is perceived loss of efficacy; Caregiver Strain & Distress is the accumulated burden on the poster and household.

Definitions:

- **Anger & Outrage:** Hot moral anger aimed at scammers, complicit companies, or institutions — indignation, resentment, and condemnation over the deception of elders. Distinguish from Frustration & Exasperation: anger targets a culprit; frustration is exasperation at blocked redress or an unpersuadable parent. Distinguish from Justice & Injustice, which adds a claim of being wronged and a demand for redress on top of the emotion.

- **Protective Concern:** Worry on behalf of an at-risk elder — usually the poster's own parent or grandparent — framed as wanting to shield or rescue them from an ongoing or looming scam; includes rescue-resolve ("determination") toward a brainwashed family member. Distinguish from Warning & Caution: concern is directed at one's own elder, warning at the public. Distinguish from Fear & Alarm (acute dread, not diffuse worry) and from Caregiver Strain & Distress (the toll on the poster, not worry for the elder).

- **Warning & Caution:** An alerting, admonitory stance: the post is framed as a caution to other readers — "beware of this scheme" — or expresses settled watchful wariness, from neutral PSA reposts to intensified public warnings. Distinguish from Protective Concern (outward-facing vs. inward-facing) and from Doubt & Suspicion: caution is a settled wary stance, suspicion is unresolved questioning of whether something is a scam.

- **Frustration & Exasperation:** Worn-down exasperation and irritation — at a parent who will not stop buying 保健品, at companies and hotlines that stonewall complaints, at scams that keep recurring. This includes adult children venting after failed persuasion or refund attempts. Distinguish from Anger & Outrage (moral indignation at a culprit) and from Helplessness & Desperation, where the poster has given up on efficacy altogether.

- **Helplessness & Desperation:** Powerlessness and despair: the poster feels unable to stop the scam, recover the money, or get through to the victim, and is at the end of their rope — parents irretrievably committed to a 传销 scheme, savings gone with no recourse. Distinguish from Frustration & Exasperation (still trying but thwarted vs. no efficacy left) and from Caregiver Strain & Distress (perceived impossibility vs. accumulated burden). Panic codes as Fear & Alarm, not here.

- **Doubt & Suspicion:** Epistemic uncertainty: suspecting an offer or product is a scam, confusion about what actually happened, or stunned incredulity at a scheme — the whole "求鉴定 / is this a scam?" family, including bafflement at a parent's purchase. Distinguish from Betrayal & Distrust (post-confirmation loss of trust) and from Warning & Caution (settled wariness rather than open questioning).

- **Fear & Alarm:** Acute dread, anxiety, alarm, or panic about what the scam has done or may do — fear of financial ruin, health damage, a 碰瓷 setup, or an unfolding fraud escalating. Distinguish from Protective Concern (diffuse other-directed worry vs. acute dread) and from Urgency (time pressure to act, which need not involve dread).

- **Promotional Appeal:** A persuasive, selling or defending register: hope, enthusiasm, reassurance, gratitude, and miracle/wealth appeals used to promote a product, company, treatment, or opportunity — often the scam itself (shill or testimonial posts), or a pitch riding on a warning frame. Distinguish from Relief & Vindication (product praise vs. resolution of one's own ordeal); hybrid "warning with promotion" posts carry both this code and Warning & Caution.

- **Grief & Hurt:** Sorrow, mourning, and emotional wounds: grieving a death or devastating loss connected to the scam, or deep relational hurt within the family over the deception. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (loss-focused sorrow vs. lack of efficacy); "hurt" — wounded feelings between kin — sits here rather than with anger.

- **Urgency:** A now-or-never framing: the poster stresses that the scam is happening right now and immediate action — intervention, refunds, warnings, arrests — is needed. Distinguish from Fear & Alarm: urgency is time pressure to act, not dread of outcomes. It is a tone that can accompany warnings, anger, or help requests alike.

- **Justice & Injustice:** A wronged-party framing: naming what happened as an injustice or persecution and demanding accountability, redress, or punishment — 维权 posts, defiance, appeals to regulators and media. Distinguish from Anger & Outrage: justice labels assert a wrong and a claim to redress, not merely hot emotion (the two typically co-occur, each coded separately).

- **Betrayal & Distrust:** The rupture of trust: feeling betrayed by a trusted party — bank staff, insurers, phone carriers, relatives, longtime friends — and a resulting generalized loss of trust in people or institutions (存款变保单 posts are the archetype). Distinguish from Doubt & Suspicion: distrust is a post-hoc verdict after the deception is confirmed; suspicion is pre-verdict uncertainty.

- **Regret & Moral Reflection:** Self-directed moral emotion: regret or embarrassment over having been fooled or having failed to intervene, remorse, retrospective self-examination, and moral dilemmas about the right thing to do (e.g., whether to tell a scammed mother the truth). Distinguish from Grief & Hurt (self-blame and moral conflict vs. sorrow over loss) and from Doubt & Suspicion (moral uncertainty about the right action vs. factual uncertainty about whether it is a scam).

- **Caregiver Strain & Distress:** The emotional and family toll of the scam on the poster and household: distress, family conflict, burnout, and exhaustion from managing a scammed elder. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (accumulated burden vs. perceived impossibility) and from Protective Concern (toll on the poster vs. worry for the elder).

- **Ridicule & Sarcasm:** A mocking, satirical, or sarcastic register: deriding scammers, gullible buying, or the absurdity of a scheme, including satire written in a scammer's voice (e.g., a mock 悔过书). Distinguish from Anger & Outrage: contemptuous humor, not hot indignation. A sarcastic warning carries both this code and Warning & Caution.

- **Empathy & Sympathy:** Compassion for scam victims other than (or beyond) one's own family — feeling for the defrauded elders described in the post. Distinguish from Protective Concern: empathy is other-oriented fellow-feeling toward victims in general; concern is protective worry about a specific elder the poster is responsible for.

- **Relief & Vindication:** Positive resolution feelings: relief that a scam was stopped or losses recovered, and vindication at being proven right after a long fight. Distinguish from Justice & Injustice (redress achieved vs. redress demanded) and from Promotional Appeal (resolution of one's own ordeal vs. product praise).

- **Help-Seeking:** The post's affective register is a plea: the poster frames their situation as a distress-toned request for assistance, advice, or intervention from the community. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (a plea directed at readers vs. an expression of powerlessness); this is the emotional register, not the Help & Advice Seeking post genre.

## 13. Poster Is First-Hand Participant (binary)

- Yes (First-Hand Participant)
- No (Third-Party Retelling)

Code exactly one value for every post; this variable is never multi-coded and never left empty — the coder always decides yes or no.

Note: The dividing line is personal stake in the specific incident, not narrative voice. Unlike the Reddit codebook's is_participant, Yes here is broader: it covers not only posters who were the scam target themselves but also family members writing about their own household's case, and there is no "unspecified" option.

Definitions:

- **Yes (First-Hand Participant):** The poster is personally inside the incident — either the scam target themself, or a close family member (child, in-law, spouse) describing their own family's ongoing or past case. Distinguish from No (Third-Party Retelling): the incident must involve the poster's own family or self, not someone else's case they are reporting on.

- **No (Third-Party Retelling):** The poster recounts a scam they were not personally involved in — reposted news reports, media exposés, legal-case write-ups, didactic stories about other people's victimization, or general public warnings. Distinguish from Yes (First-Hand Participant): the victim is a neighbor, a stranger, a news subject, or an aggregate of 老人 in general, and the poster has no personal stake in the specific case.

## 14. Help from Comments (multi-select (up to three); N/A when comments give no codable help)

- Reporting & Complaint-Channel Advice
- Safety Warnings & Prevention
- Verification & Appraisal
- Legal Action & Evidence Advice
- Scam Analysis & Identification
- Victim Corroboration
- Fund Recovery & Refund Advice
- Resources & Practical Alternatives
- Disengagement & Exit Advice
- Family Intervention & Financial Controls
- Promotion, Spam & Scam Defense
- Emotional Support
- No Substantive Help
- N/A

Code every distinct help function the comment thread performs, up to three. Do not force a code when the captured comments do not support one.

Note: Several codes sit close together and are split by function, not topic. "Go file a complaint with the police/12315/regulator" is Reporting & Complaint-Channel Advice; "sue them / review the contract / preserve evidence" is Legal Action & Evidence Advice. "This is a scam, here is how it works" (commenters supply the verdict) is Scam Analysis & Identification; "get it appraised / check the approval number first" (commenters send the poster to check) is Verification & Appraisal; generic "don't buy, beware high returns" is Safety Warnings & Prevention. Getting money already paid back is Fund Recovery & Refund Advice; cutting off further loss (withdraw, cancel, block, escape) is Disengagement & Exit Advice. "This happened to my family too" is Victim Corroboration; comfort without shared victimization is Emotional Support. Compound comment threads carry every function they contain — cross-list rather than choosing a winner.

Definitions:

- **Reporting & Complaint-Channel Advice:** Commenters advise escalating through official complaint channels — police report, 12315/315 hotlines, market/tourism/commerce regulators, cyberspace authorities, carriers, insurers, platform complaint mechanisms, or the media. The test is channel-oriented advice ("file a complaint with X"). Distinguish from Legal Action & Evidence Advice: lawsuits, contracts, and evidence for court go there, not here.
- **Safety Warnings & Prevention:** Generic, forward-looking cautionary advice: don't buy, beware high returns / TV shopping / a named brand, use safer transaction practices, general scam-prevention guidance. Distinguish from Scam Analysis & Identification: warnings caution against a class of behavior, while analysis diagnoses the specific scheme at hand.
- **Verification & Appraisal:** Commenters direct the poster to independently CHECK the item, claim, or entity before concluding or acting — professional 鉴定 of jade/collectibles/medicines, retesting, checking official approvals or registrations, verifying a counterparty or project, or searching for information. Distinguish from Scam Analysis & Identification: there the commenters supply the verdict themselves; here they send the poster to get it checked.
- **Legal Action & Evidence Advice:** Advice on legal remedies and their groundwork: lawsuits, contract or policy review, joint action by victims, preserving evidence of fraud, and assessments of legal-recourse prospects (including "limited recourse"). Court/contract-oriented — distinguish from Reporting & Complaint-Channel Advice, which covers administrative complaint channels.
- **Scam Analysis & Identification:** Commenters diagnose the situation themselves: confirming it is a scam, explaining the mechanism (Ponzi structure, non-standard P2P custody), or clarifying what the product or scheme actually is. Distinguish from Victim Corroboration: analysis is third-party diagnosis without personal victimization; corroboration is first-hand "me too" testimony.
- **Victim Corroboration:** Other victims or their families testify in the comments that they were hit by the same scheme, corroborating the poster's account and sometimes pooling information (recovery estimates, arrest claims, support-group formation). A distinctive Tieba function: threads become de facto victim registries. Distinguish from Emotional Support: corroboration confirms shared victimization; support comforts without it.
- **Fund Recovery & Refund Advice:** Advice or assessments aimed at getting money back that was already paid: refunds, returns, policy surrender to recoup premiums, recovery-prospect estimates — including pessimistic "no recovery" verdicts. Distinguish from Disengagement & Exit Advice, which stops further loss rather than clawing back past loss.
- **Resources & Practical Alternatives:** Commenters supply a positive substitute or practical resource: a cheaper or legitimate alternative to the scam product, a workaround for a refused service, or redirection to proper medical care (regular-hospital evaluation, dialysis support, nursing-home resources). Distinguish from Safety Warnings & Prevention: a warning says "don't"; this code offers "use this instead."
- **Disengagement & Exit Advice:** Advice to sever the ongoing scam relationship and stop further loss: withdraw remaining funds, escape a 传销 environment, cancel contracts or services, block the scammers' numbers or networks, confront the upline to exit. Distinguish from Fund Recovery & Refund Advice: disengagement is forward-looking severance, not recovering money already lost.
- **Family Intervention & Financial Controls:** Advice on how the family should manage the scammed elder and their money: separating or controlling finances, educating the elder, direct family intervention, caregiving-arrangement boundaries. Targets the family's management of the elder, not the scammer.
- **Promotion, Spam & Scam Defense:** Anti-helpful comment content: unverified product promotions and spam posted into the thread, and comments defending the scheme's legitimacy. Serves the seller/scammer side or exploits the audience — broader than well-meant-but-risky advice. When a "Mixed" thread contains both warnings and promotion, code both this and the helpful function.
- **Emotional Support:** Comfort, sympathy, and solidarity offered to the poster, including support-group formation among affected families. Purely affective — code the instrumental content of the same thread under its own code. Distinguish from Victim Corroboration: support does not assert shared victimization.
- **No Substantive Help:** Residual code for a captured response record that contains comments but delivers no substantive help. Use only when no help function (not even Emotional Support) is present in those comments.
- **N/A:** Leave N/A when no usable comment response was captured or when the available comment material is only silence/noise and cannot support a comment-help judgment. Do not force a code onto absent evidence. Reserve No Substantive Help for cases where a response record exists and shows that commenters responded without delivering help.

## 15. Loss Recovery Status (single-select)

- No (Not Recovered)
- Unknown (Outcome Unstated)
- Partial
- Prevented
- Yes (Recovered)
- Claimed Recovered (Unverified)
- N/A

Code exactly one value per post; this variable is never multi-coded. Use Partial, Prevented, Yes, or Claimed Recovered only when the post gives explicit outcome evidence.

Note: The confusable pair is No vs. Unknown. No requires positive evidence that the loss stands (a refusal to refund, an unreachable seller, a collapsed scheme, money written off); mere silence about the aftermath is Unknown. Separately, Prevented is not a recovery at all — the extraction never completed — so do not code Yes when family, bank, or police stopped the scam before money left.

Definitions:

- **No (Not Recovered):** The post gives positive evidence that the loss stands: money or property was not returned, and no successful recovery is described (refund refused, seller unreachable, scheme collapsed, loss written off). Distinguish from Unknown (Outcome Unstated): if the post simply never says what happened afterward, code Unknown. Distinguish from Partial: even a small refund moves the case to Partial.

- **Unknown (Outcome Unstated):** The post does not reveal whether the loss was recovered — the outcome is unstated, or the case is still open and unresolved at posting time. This includes seeking-help posts written mid-incident, warnings that end before any resolution, and third-party stories with no follow-up. Tieba's raw label is "unknown" where the Reddit codebook used "unspecified"; the concept is identical.

- **Partial:** Some but not all of the extracted value came back — a partial refund after negotiation, surrender value on a mis-sold policy at a loss, or return of a fraction of deposits from a collapsed scheme. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): recovery is materially incomplete. Distinguish from No (Not Recovered): at least something was returned. This code has no Reddit analog (Reddit was strictly yes/no/unspecified).

- **Prevented:** The attempted scam was intercepted before the loss was completed — a relative, bank, police, or the target themself stopped it — so no loss existed to recover. Includes blocked transfers, fraudulent loan applications caught in time, and purchases stopped by family. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): nothing was ever recovered because extraction never completed; this records successful prevention as its own analytically distinct outcome.

- **Yes (Recovered):** The post credibly reports that the loss was returned in full or essentially in full — a refund obtained, money returned after complaint or consumer-protection intervention, goods reversed. Distinguish from Partial (only some funds back), from Claimed Recovered (Unverified) (recovery merely asserted, not verified), and from Prevented (the loss never completed).

- **Claimed Recovered (Unverified):** Someone in the post asserts the money came back, but the claim is unverified and may itself be part of a scheme — e.g., a victim repeating a Ponzi "you got your principal back" narrative, or a recovery-service promoter touting its own success. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): the coder records the assertion without endorsing it; the "recovery" may be scheme-internal paper gains or promotional puffery.

- **N/A:** Leave this variable N/A when the post contains no extraction episode whose outcome could be tracked — general discussion or debate posts (e.g., 碰瓷/扶老人 ethics threads), complaints with no monetary loss to the elder, or PSA/科普 content that never narrates a specific victim's loss. Do not force a code onto a post that has no loss in it.


## Coding notes

- **Variable 1 discipline:** Scam Relevance is coded even when other variables are hard to fill; a post coded "No (Not a Scam)" or "Unknown (Undetermined)" can still carry purpose, category, and emotion codes describing the suspected episode.
- **5A vs 5B distinction:** 5A records the channel through which the scheme first reached the elder (the X in "X to Y"); 5B records what the elder was brought to do (the Y). Always code one of each when the route is stated.
- **7 (Red Flags) vs 3 (Category):** category is the scheme type; red flags are the warning signs the post itself evidences. Do not derive red flags from the category alone — each coded red flag needs support in the text.
- **12 (Emotional Frame):** code the one or two DOMINANT frames of the poster (not of commenters); never more than two.
- **Multi-select fields:** 3 (up to 2), 4 (up to 3), 5A (up to 2), 5B (up to 3), 5 combined (up to 4), 6 (up to 2), 7 (up to 5), 8 (up to 3), 9 (up to 4), 11 (up to 2), 14 (up to 3). Do not force extra codes to fill a quota — code what the post evidences.
- **N/A discipline:** for 11 (Party Intervened), use N/A when no third-party institution appears; for 14 (Help from Comments), use N/A when no usable comment response was captured. Never leave a variable blank — output "N/A" instead.

## Output format

For the post you are given, output ONE JSON object, no other text, with EXACTLY these 15 keys. Every value is an array of code names copied verbatim from this codebook (single-select variables get a one-element array; use ["N/A"] where the variable does not apply):

- Unspecified Sales Contact: Residual: the post records a sales pitch, promotion, advice, or inducement without naming any venue, medium, or relationship for the first contact. Use only when no channel information exists; any named venue, medium, or tie moves the row to a substantive code.
- Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation: Contact through interactive online spaces: WeChat/QQ/social-media groups, friend requests, private chats, online lecture groups, viral rumors, P2P and online lending platforms. Two-way interaction distinguishes it from Mass-Media Advertising; selling by a live host codes to Livestream & Short-Video Commerce; a self-initiated storefront transaction codes to E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform.
- Offline Recruitment & Investment Solicitation: Contact by recruiters or company representatives soliciting investment, project participation, or scheme membership, where no family/friend vector and no online medium is specified. If the recruiter is kin or a friend, code Family, Friend & Acquaintance Channel instead; if recruitment happens online, code Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation.
- Telemarketing & Phone Contact: Contact by telephone under the caller's own, non-disguised identity: cold sales or prize calls, 10085/operator plan calls, follow-up calls after a previous purchase, repeated landline calls. Distinguish from Impersonation & Pretext Contact: a fake-police or fake-son call codes there, not here — identity disguise beats the phone medium.
- Tour & Travel-Group Funnel: Contact occurs inside a tourism setting: low-price or free tours (低价团), tour-group shopping stops, tour guides, tourist shops, or extra-fee coercion on paid tours. The trip itself is the funnel. Distinguish from Free-Gift, Prize & Seminar Funnel, where the lure event is local, and from Family, Friend & Acquaintance Channel when relatives supplied the trip.
- Impersonation & Pretext Contact: First contact is made under a false identity or staged pretext: fake 公检法 (police/court) calls, fake kin, fake utility or company employees, WeChat impersonation, buyers posing as someone they are not, staged incident scenarios. Identity disguise takes precedence over medium — a fake-police phone call codes here, not to Telemarketing & Phone Contact. Distinguish from Family, Friend & Acquaintance Channel, where a real relationship is exploited rather than fabricated.
- E-commerce & Remote Purchase Platform: Contact begins with a self-initiated online storefront transaction — e-commerce listings, online phone purchases, mini-program purchases — that turns out fraudulent or seeds further exploitation. Distinguish from Online Groups, Chat & Platform Solicitation (the elder is solicited in a chat/group rather than shopping); when the purchased product or account later leaks charges, dual-code with Provider, Store & Existing-Account Channel.
- Livestream & Short-Video Commerce: Contact via livestream shopping rooms or short-video commerce, often with parasocial "family" bonding between host and elderly viewers. The real-time performative selling and host relationship separate it from static Online Groups/Chat and from broadcast Mass-Media ads.

B. Commitment

- Purchase: The elder is moved to buy goods or product-like services — one-off, repeated, or bulk — including overpriced, counterfeit, or medically useless products (保健品, devices, medicines bought as boxed products, jewelry, collectibles, water purifiers, memberships-as-cards). Money-for-goods distinguishes it from Direct Payment & Transfer (money with nothing bought) and Investment & Deposit (money placed for returns); insurance "purchases" code to Contract, Policy, Loan & Property Signing, and forced or coerced purchases code to Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction.
- Investment & Deposit: The elder commits money expecting returns: investments, deposits into schemes, platforms, or apps, shares, equity agreements, tokens, or fund placements — usually escalating and repeated. Financial instruments code here even when the label says "purchase" (shares, equity, tokens); physical collectibles stay in Purchase, and money handed over with no return story is Direct Payment & Transfer.
- Contract, Policy, Loan & Property Signing: The elder signs a binding instrument: insurance policies (the 存款变保单 pattern), service contracts and plan signups, loan or mortgage documents, powers of attorney, property transfers, or business setups. The harm is locked in by a signed arrangement rather than a money handover. Distinguish from Investment & Deposit: a bank fixed-term deposit without a policy codes there.
- Direct Payment & Transfer: The elder hands over money as such — bank transfers, cash handoffs, fees, top-ups, upfront treatment payments, or lending to a person — without receiving goods or an investment vehicle. No goods (Purchase) and no return narrative (Investment & Deposit); payments extracted by coercion or without authorization code to Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction. The elder lending money out codes here; the elder signing as borrower codes to Contract/Loan Signing.
- Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration: The elder is moved into continued interaction short of paying or joining: attending lectures, meetings, seminars, or staged sales shows, downloading apps, visiting websites or clinics, performing tasks, or migrating to a private channel such as a WeChat group. Often the mid-funnel step. Distinguish from Joining, Membership & Recruitment: attending and interacting, without organizational affiliation.
- Joining, Membership & Recruitment: The elder affiliates with a scheme or organization: joining 传销/pyramid schemes, paying memberships, becoming a downline recruiter, enrolling, or entering a facility or program. Affiliative commitment distinguishes it from Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration; recruiting others (downlines) always codes here.
- Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction: Money or property is taken without a voluntary commitment: theft by trusted persons, unauthorized deductions and pension transfers, hidden recurring or data charges, forced/coerced purchases and fees, forced enrollment, blackmail, staged compensation demands. Absence of genuine consent separates it from every voluntary-commitment code; rows mixing voluntary and imposed elements are dual-coded (e.g. policy purchase + automatic deductions).
- Treatment, Regimen & Ritual Uptake: The elder undergoes a health treatment, procedure, regimen, or superstition ritual — therapy courses, unproven drug regimens, restrictive diets, surgeries, clinic visits, luck-removal rituals. The commitment is the elder's body and behavior, not only money. Head-noun rule: buying medicine as a boxed product stays in Purchase; a course, regimen, or session undergone codes here.
- Relationship & Grooming Commitment: The elder is drawn into an emotional, romantic, or parasocial bond — private WeChat grooming, courtship, marriage, staged intimacy — as the commitment the scam runs on. The bond itself is the secured commitment; downstream money events are dual-coded. Distinguish from Engagement, Attendance & Channel Migration, which is transactional interaction without emotional bonding.
- Travel & Inspection Trip: The elder commits to traveling — "project" site inspections, overseas trips, or travel bundled into recruitment — as part of the scam's escalation. Distinguish from Tour & Travel-Group Funnel on the channel axis: there tourism is how contact is made; here the trip is a demanded commitment inside the route.
- Access & Disclosure: The commitment is granting entry or exposing information: verifying accounts, letting scammers into the home, or having personal/SIM data captured through a compromised product. The extracted good is access or data, not money — covert data capture stays here rather than in Coerced, Hidden & Unauthorized Extraction (money/property outcome).
- Contemplated / No Commitment: The route stops before any commitment: the elder was contacted or is merely considering the pitch, typically in seeking-verification posts written pre-commitment, or scam calls reaching the household with no action taken. Distinguished from every other commitment code by the absence of any completed action; the contact channel is still coded normally in column A.
- N/A: Leave both columns N/A when the post contains no contact-to-commitment episode at all — general discussion, policy commentary, or PSA posts with no scammer reaching a specific elder (e.g. a debate thread on whether to help a fallen elder given 碰瓷 risk). If any concrete route is narrated, however thin, code it instead of N/A.

6. Asset Extracted (single or multi-select (up to two))

- Money
- Health
- Property
- Contract or Debt Obligation
- Labor
- Data
- N/A

Code up to two distinct extracted assets. Use a second code only when the text supports a separate asset type (e.g. Money + Health in 保健品 cases, Money + Property in theft cases). Never code more than two.

Note: The four "non-cash" codes are separated by what actually leaves the elder. Funds already paid out = Money; a specific tangible thing or real-asset interest handed over = Property; a still-binding legal commitment to pay in the future = Contract or Debt Obligation; personal information that enables further fraud = Data. Bodily harm from the scheme is Health, and effort/time/care sunk into it is Labor — neither is a substitute for Money when payments also occurred; code both.

Definitions:

- Money: Direct financial extraction — cash, bank transfers, deposits, purchase payments for fake or overpriced products, investment/传销 buy-ins, insurance premiums, and unauthorized fees or phone charges (吸费, 乱扣费). This is the dominant asset and covers funds that have already left the elder's hands, whatever their form. Distinguish from Contract or Debt Obligation: money already paid is Money; a liability for future payments is the contract/debt code. Distinguish from Data: bank INFORMATION is Data; funds later moved with it are Money.

- Health: The elder's bodily health harmed or put at risk as a cost of the scam — ingesting fake cures or 三无 products, undergoing pseudoscientific 理疗, or forgoing real medical treatment on the scammer's advice. This may be coded alongside Money, which covers the payment while Health covers the physical-harm dimension. Distinguish from Labor: Health is what the scheme does to the victim's body, not effort the victim expends.

- Property: Physical goods, valuables, or real assets taken from or ceded by the elder — jewelry, phones, cash-adjacent valuables stolen in trust-building burglaries, pets, or housing/real-estate interests signed away. Distinguish from Money: Property is a non-fungible tangible asset (a jade bracelet handed over is Property; the cash paid for one is Money).

- Contract or Debt Obligation: A binding financial commitment imposed on the elder through deception — unwanted 存款变保单-style policies, bundled multi-year telecom plans, subscriptions, or loan/debt liabilities taken out in the elder's name. The extracted harm is the ongoing obligation itself, even if little cash has left yet. Distinguish from Property: no tangible asset changes hands; what is extracted is a legal commitment.

- Labor: The elder's (or family's) time, effort, care work, or emotional labor extracted by the scheme — years of sustained 传销 participation, caregiving and affective investment drained by romance/marriage fraud or exploitative kin. Distinguish from Money: Labor is expended effort and time, not funds; when both are drained, code both.

- Data: Personal information extracted from the elder — identity details, phone numbers/WeChat contacts harvested in marketing schemes, or bank/account information usable for further fraud. Data is the informational enabler, not the funds themselves; when identity data is used to saddle the elder with a fraudulent loan, code the data as Data and the liability as Contract or Debt Obligation.

- N/A: Leave N/A when the scam episode never reaches (or even aims at) extraction of any identifiable asset from an elder — e.g. general discussion threads (碰瓷/扶不扶 debates), harassment or nuisance calls with no loss described, and complaint posts where nothing was actually taken or committed. Do not force a code onto posts that describe no extracted or targeted value.

7. Red Flags (multi-select; max five)

- False Health Claims & Endangerment
- Unrealistic Promises & Scheme Mechanics
- Faked Identity, Authority & Proof
- Product & Legitimacy Deception
- Pressure & Escalating Demands
- Covert Terms & Charges
- Non-Delivery, Withdrawal Failure & Vanishing
- Grooming, Isolation & Bait
- Targeting & Access Exploitation
- N/A

Code every warning sign the post itself identifies, one code per distinct sign, up to five. One code fits a simple episode; reserve four or five for genuinely dense episodes. Do not infer red flags from the scam category alone.

Note: Each code is a family grouping the former finer-grained red-flag codes listed under it; the constituent definitions are retained verbatim below as coding guidance. Code at the family level.

Definitions:

- False Health Claims & Endangerment (groups former codes: Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims; Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine):
  - Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims: A product or treatment is claimed, without evidence, to cure, prevent, or treat disease — cure-all (包治百病) and no-side-effect claims, pseudoscientific mechanisms (quantum/nanotech, 9000V therapy, 祖传秘方), and temporary effects passed off as cures. This is the signature red flag of 保健品/会销 health-product scams. Distinguish from Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine: this code captures the false benefit claim itself, not practices that actively endanger health.
  - Dangerous Health Practices & Undermining Medicine: The scam actively endangers the elder's health: inducing them to stop medication or refuse hospitals, unsafe procedures and dosages, harmful regimens, undeclared drugs, unsafe devices, and caregiver medical neglect or abuse. Distinguish from Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims: this codes physical endangerment, not the false benefit claim.

- Unrealistic Promises & Scheme Mechanics (groups former codes: Unrealistic Financial Promise; Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics):
  - Unrealistic Financial Promise: The scammer offers returns, payouts, buybacks, subsidies, or benefits that are implausibly favorable or contractually empty — guaranteed high interest, double-digit monthly returns, promised buybacks, verbal promises never honored. Promises of money or material benefit only; health-cure promises go to Miracle-Cure & Pseudoscience Claims. Distinguish from Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics: this codes the pitch content, not the scheme structure behind it.
  - Pyramid, Ponzi & Illegal-Fundraising Mechanics: Structural red flags of unsustainable schemes: entry fees and pay-to-join, downline/recruitment hierarchies (传销) and recruitment-funded returns, inventory stockpiling, new money paying earlier investors, and unlicensed mass fundraising (非法集资), including 资本运作-style jargon. Distinguish from Unrealistic Financial Promise: this codes the scheme's funding architecture, not the payoff pitched to each victim.

- Faked Identity, Authority & Proof (groups former codes: False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials; Impersonation & False Identity; Fabricated Social Proof & Staged Demonstrations):
  - False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials: Borrowed or fabricated backing legitimizes the scam: fake government affiliation, projects, policies, or notices; official, celebrity, or divine endorsements; insider-access claims; forged certificates, stamps, policies, and company records. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: this codes fabricated BACKING and papers, not posing as a person or organization in interaction.
  - Impersonation & False Identity: The scammer pretends to be someone or something they are not: a family member, a utility/telecom/bank employee, a lawyer or military official, or a fabricated persona — including fear pretexts delivered under that guise. Distinguish from Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access: here the relationship is fake; there, a real one is abused.
  - Fabricated Social Proof & Staged Demonstrations: Manufactured peer or outcome evidence: paid shills (托), actors posing as patients, staged testimonials and prize reactions, advertorials disguised as independent content, and anecdotal recovery or income stories used as proof. Distinguish from False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials: the faked proof here comes from peers, patients, or outcomes, not from institutions or officials.

- Product & Legitimacy Deception (groups former codes: Unverifiable Legitimacy; Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods; Deceptive Pricing & Extreme Markup):
  - Unverifiable Legitimacy: The seller, product, or story fails basic verification: unknown or obscure brands, no regulatory approval or licence, no manufacturer details or receipts, shell or brand-new companies, vague or unverifiable assets and claims, inconsistent identifying details. The test is absence — "cannot confirm it is real." Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods (the item is proven fake) and from False Authority, Endorsement & Forged Credentials (credentials are affirmatively fabricated rather than simply absent).
  - Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods: The thing sold or delivered is not what it purports to be: counterfeits, refurbished-as-new devices, worthless or nonfunctional goods, product substitutions, brand confusion, and sham financial instruments (fake shares, accounts, cards). Distinguish from Unverifiable Legitimacy: here the item is affirmatively fake or misrepresented, not merely impossible to verify.
  - Deceptive Pricing & Extreme Markup: Price-value deception: inflated or fake "original" prices, fake discounts, extreme markups on low-value goods, exaggerated value or rarity, tourist-venue markups, and elder-specific surcharges. Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods: this codes what the item COSTS relative to its worth, not what the item IS; the two frequently co-occur and are then co-assigned.

- Pressure & Escalating Demands (groups former codes: Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals; Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands):
  - Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals: Compliance is forced through urgency, repetition, aggression, or manufactured fear — high-pressure sales and upselling, forced purchases, threats (abandonment, prosecution, blackmail), harassment, health fearmongering, and fabricated emergencies. Distinguish from Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination: this code works through aversive force and fear, not positive affect.
  - Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands: The payment pattern itself is the red flag: repeated or escalating fees, deposits, and transfers; pay-more demands; full prepayment; debt-funded participation; excessive or bulk buying; and irregular channels such as cash couriers or off-platform payment. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges: these payments are openly demanded, not covertly taken.

- Covert Terms & Charges (groups former codes: Hidden Terms & Covert Charges; Financial Product Bait-and-Switch):
  - Hidden Terms & Covert Charges: Material facts or money are taken or withheld covertly: hidden surrender fees, lock-ins and penalties, legal-document traps and over-broad authorizations, unauthorized or automatic deductions, preinstalled fee software and trojans, unexplained charges, evidence destruction. Distinguish from Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands (payments demanded openly) and from Financial Product Bait-and-Switch (the product's identity is misrepresented at sale).
  - Financial Product Bait-and-Switch: A financial product is sold as something it is not — the classic 存款变保单 pattern: insurance disguised as a bank deposit, savings product, or investment, with terms misrepresented at sale and verbal promises contradicting the written contract. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges (terms merely buried in fine print) and from Unrealistic Financial Promise (implausible returns without product mislabeling).

- Non-Delivery, Withdrawal Failure & Vanishing: After payment, the promised goods, services, payouts, or withdrawals never materialize — refund refusals, frozen accounts, suspended withdrawals, endless delays and excuses, unreachable sellers, companies that disappear or abscond. Distinguish from Counterfeit, Sham & Misrepresented Goods (a fake thing DOES arrive) and from Escalating & Irregular Payment Demands (demands for more money rather than failure to deliver).

- Grooming, Isolation & Bait (groups former codes: Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination; Isolation, Secrecy & Warning Resistance; Free-Gift, Prize & Low-Price Bait):
  - Emotional Grooming & Indoctrination: Affection, belonging, or ideology is cultivated to disable skepticism: emotional manipulation and storytelling, brainwashing (洗脑), motivational and success-culture indoctrination, fictive kinship, and overfriendly trust-building toward isolated elders. Distinguish from Pressure, Coercion & Fear Appeals: this works through positive affect and belief change, not fear or force.
  - Isolation, Secrecy & Warning Resistance: The elder is cut off from corrective voices: secrecy demands, family isolation, movement to private channels, elderly-only venues, concealment of losses from family, and disregard of family, bank, or police warnings. Distinguish from Hidden Terms & Covert Charges: here the victim is hidden from others; there, facts are hidden from the victim.
  - Free-Gift, Prize & Low-Price Bait: Small up-front material lures — free gifts, checkups (免费体检), tours, trials, prize wins, below-cost prices, or small initial investment profits — hook the elder into much larger extraction. The lure is actually delivered up front. Distinguish from Unrealistic Financial Promise: that code covers large FUTURE benefits merely promised, not small freebies handed over now.

- Targeting & Access Exploitation (groups former codes: Elder-Vulnerability Targeting; Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access; Unsolicited or Stranger Approach):
  - Elder-Vulnerability Targeting: The scam deliberately selects elders or exploits an elder-specific incapacity — illiteracy, cognitive impairment, inability to read contracts, unclear consent, elders-only venues, pension (养老) pretexts, or elder-only surcharges. Distinguish from Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access: this codes WHO is targeted and which incapacity is exploited, not which real relationship is abused.
  - Exploitation of Trusted Relationships & Insider Access: A genuinely trusted party — a child or relative, caregiver, bank insider, or befriended acquaintance — abuses real access to the elder or their assets: thefts by children, caregiver abuse, insider transfers, copied keys after befriending, recruitment through one's own family. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: the relationship or access here is real, not faked.
  - Unsolicited or Stranger Approach: The contact or transaction is initiated out of the blue by an unknown party: stranger solicitation, unsolicited promotions, calls, or official-sounding notices, services activated without request, dealings with complete strangers. Distinguish from Impersonation & False Identity: this codes the UNINVITED nature of the contact itself, not who the contact pretends to be.

- N/A: Use only when the post identifies no warning sign at all — e.g., a bare loss report or general discussion that describes no deceptive claim, manipulative tactic, structural irregularity, or exploitation cue. N/A is an exception, not a fallback.

8. Response & Protective Action (multi-select (up to four))

- Seek Help & Advice
- Warn & Educate Others
- Verify & Investigate
- Report & Legal Action
- Seek Remedy & Recover
- Dissuade & Confront Elder
- Take Control & Secure
- Public Appeal & Collective Action
- Accept Comply
- Continue Persist In Belief
- Promote Advocate Scheme
- Reject Refuse
- Stop Disengage
- Distress & Regret
- Other Respond
- N/A

This variable merges the former "8. Elder / Family Response" and "9. Informal Security Work" into a single variable. Code every distinct response or protective, investigative, remedial, or accountability action that appears in the episode, up to four. Do not force an extra code merely to fill the cap. Code each distinct actor-action pair: "family reported, victim believed" takes one code per actor.

Note: Codes marked as merged group the former codes listed under them; the constituent definitions are retained verbatim below as coding guidance, including their actor and channel distinctions. Code at the merged-code level.

Definitions:

- Seek Help & Advice (merges: Seek Help & Advice; Consult):
  - Seek Help & Advice (former Response code): Turning to the Tieba community, peers, or other informal sources for advice, identification, guidance, emotional support, or practical help — including the elder disclosing her own plight. This is the signature Tieba response: the post itself is often the help-seeking act by the victim or their adult child. Distinguish from Question Doubt & Verify: asking "what should I do?" versus asking "is this real / what is it worth?"; help sought from police or regulators belongs under Report To Authorities & Legal Action.
  - Consult (former Informal Security Work code): Turning to the online community — this Tieba or other forums/groups — for advice, judgment, sense-making, or help with what to do next, including open discussion of the poster's own case. Distinguish from Warn: discussion seeks input for one's own case, Warn broadcasts to protect others. Professional legal advice is Legal, not Consult.

- Warn & Educate Others (merges: Warn Others & Public Exposure; Warn; Fraud Education):
  - Warn Others & Public Exposure (former Response code): Alerting people beyond the household: publicly exposing the scheme, company, streamer, or suspect; warning other consumers, investors, villagers, or viewers; enlisting media; or official public-education warnings relayed in news/PSA posts. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action: the audience here is the PUBLIC, not an enforcement institution. Mirror image of Promote Advocate Scheme.
  - Warn (former Informal Security Work code): Making the scam publicly known to protect others — online or public exposure (曝光), forum warnings, public complaints, and warnings issued through media, police, lawyers, or consumer associations. Distinguish from Fraud Education: Warn flags a particular scam or scammer, Education teaches general anti-fraud practice. Distinguish from Public Appeal & Advocacy: Warn protects future victims, Appeal seeks help or justice for an existing one.
  - Fraud Education (former Informal Security Work code): General anti-fraud pedagogy (科普/反诈宣传): teaching elders, families, investors, or the public how scams work and what protective practices to adopt, beyond warning about one specific scam — case-based education, investor education, police-delivered education, family education of the elder, and protective-practice advice. Distinguish from Warn: Education transfers generalizable knowledge, Warn flags a particular scam. Distinguish from Coordinate: teaching the elder to self-protect is Education; confrontation or supervision that directly interrupts a scam is Coordinate.

- Verify & Investigate (merges: Question Doubt & Verify; Investigate & Gather Evidence; Check; Document; Media Investigation):
  - Question Doubt & Verify (former Response code): The suspicious elder or (more often) a family member questions legitimacy and checks the specific transaction, product, or policy: seeking appraisal or valuation, verifying with the genuine institution (bank, carrier, water company), reviewing contracts and bills, voicing skeptical critique of the claims, or belatedly realizing the deception. Distinguish from Investigate & Gather Evidence: Verify checks one's OWN purchase/policy; Investigate probes the scheme, company, or perpetrator itself.
  - Investigate & Gather Evidence (former Response code): Active probing of the scheme, company, or perpetrator beyond checking one's own transaction: researching how the scam works, going undercover or posing as an insider, monitoring the scammer's group, analyzing the business model, or collecting and preserving evidence. Usually the adult child or a whistleblowing poster. Distinguish from Question Doubt & Verify (checking one's own purchase) and from Report To Authorities & Legal Action: investigation here is self-directed detective work, not handing the matter to institutions.
  - Check (former Informal Security Work code): Verifying facts, legitimacy, identities, products, prices, bills, or documents to establish whether a scam occurred and how — online research, appraisals (incl. 鉴定/UV inspection), regulatory-database checks, contract/bill/policy review, price analysis, or directly probing the suspect party. Distinguish from Consult: Check is fact-finding the poster or family performs themselves; asking peers for judgment is Consult (a post can carry both). Media doing the same work is Media Investigation.
  - Document (former Informal Security Work code): Collecting, recording, or preserving evidence of the scam — records, recordings, photos, videos, chat logs, bills, and financial documents — whether or not it is later submitted anywhere. Distinguish from Check: Document preserves proof for later use, Check establishes what is true. When evidence is submitted to police or a platform, also code the receiving channel.
  - Media Investigation (former Informal Security Work code): Investigative and verification work performed by journalists or media outlets as third-party actors: media investigations, undercover reporting, reporter verification, fact-checks, and news reports documenting the scam case. Distinguish from Check: same epistemic work, but the actor is professional media rather than the poster or family. Distinguish from Warn: this code covers the investigative process; the publicizing output is Warn (many episodes carry both).

- Report & Legal Action (merges: Report To Authorities & Legal Action; Report to Police; Regulatory & Platform Complaint; Legal):
  - Report To Authorities & Legal Action (former Response code): The victim, family, or victims collectively escalate to formal institutions: reporting to police or the 12315 hotline, complaining to consumer-protection bodies or regulators, reporting accounts to platforms, suing the seller, or pursuing courts, prosecutors, 维权, or divorce/compensation claims. Distinguish from Seek Remedy From Seller: Report involves third-party enforcement institutions, not direct negotiation with the counterparty; "reported to police and exposed online" labels are coded here AND under Warn Others & Public Exposure.
  - Report to Police (former Informal Security Work code): Engaging the police/criminal-justice apparatus: filing 报警/报案, calling or contacting police, submissions to police-run anti-fraud channels, police investigations, and police-coordinated stings. Distinguish from Regulatory & Platform Complaint: police are the criminal channel; 12315/315, regulators, and platforms are the administrative channel — compound episodes often carry both. Frontline bank staff calling police also carries Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention.
  - Regulatory & Platform Complaint (former Informal Security Work code): Filing formal complaints or reports through China's non-police accountability channels: consumer hotlines (12315/315), 工商局 and sector regulators, and e-commerce/app platform reporting and dispute mechanisms; bare "complained/投诉" with no stated channel defaults here. Distinguish from Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention: complaints here go to third-party oversight bodies or platform mechanisms, not to the offending company or one's own bank/insurer/carrier. Distinguish from Warn: these are formal filings, not public airing.
  - Legal (former Informal Security Work code): Engaging the formal legal system or legal professionals: lawyer consultations, hiring lawyers, lawsuits, court actions and prosecutions, legal complaints, legal-aid engagement, and legal planning. Distinguish from Report to Police: civil/court and lawyer-mediated routes versus criminal reporting. The public campaigning side of 维权 is Public Appeal & Advocacy, not Legal.

- Seek Remedy & Recover (merges: Seek Remedy From Seller; Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention; Recover):
  - Seek Remedy From Seller (former Response code): The elder or their family pursues remediation directly from the seller, provider, or scheme counterparty: demanding refunds, returns, or cancellations, requesting withdrawals from investment schemes, submitting insurance claims or challenging denials, disputing charges, confronting salespeople, or contacting customer service. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action (escalation to police/regulators/courts) and from Stop Disengage: Remedy tries to claw money back, Stop merely ceases further engagement.
  - Institution Contact & Frontline Intervention (former Informal Security Work code): Directly engaging the involved company or the elder's own service institution — bank, insurer, telecom carrier, seller's customer service — to question, complain to, or seek remedy from it, plus protective interventions initiated by frontline institution staff themselves (e.g., bank staff calling police). Distinguish from Regulatory & Platform Complaint: the counterparty here is the company or institution itself, not an oversight body. Explicit refund/return segments go to Recover.
  - Recover (former Informal Security Work code): Attempting to get money or goods back after the scam: refund requests and negotiations (incl. media-brokered), product returns, and recovery through legal aid or advocacy. Distinguish from Legal: Recover targets restitution specifically; lawsuits pursued for accountability are Legal. Distinguish from Secure Funds & Devices: recovery is after-loss, controls are before or during.

- Dissuade & Confront Elder (merges: Dissuade & Confront Elder; Coordinate):
  - Dissuade & Confront Elder (former Response code): Family members try to change the elder's behavior through persuasion, argument, confrontation, in-family warnings, restraint, or benevolent counter-trickery — the family-intervention response distinctive of the Tieba caregiver vantage point. Distinguish from Reject Refuse (refusing a demand aimed at oneself) and from Take Control & Secure Finances Care: dissuasion works through communication, whereas Secure imposes structural control over money, care, or accounts.
  - Coordinate (former Informal Security Work code): Family members and relatives intervening directly with the elder — confronting, counter-persuading, questioning, warning, advising, supervising, or monitoring them to interrupt or prevent the scam. Distinguish from Secure Funds & Devices: Coordinate is persuasion and oversight of the person; restricting their money or devices is Secure Funds & Devices. Distinguish from Care & Health Protection: Coordinate counters the scam itself, Care remediates harm already done.

- Take Control & Secure (merges: Take Control & Secure Finances Care; Secure Funds & Devices; Care & Health Protection):
  - Take Control & Secure Finances Care (former Response code): Family members impose protective structural control over the elder's money, accounts, care, or living situation: taking over finances, freezing billing, taking over caregiving, monitoring care, repaying scam-induced debts, or arranging medical/psychological help. Distinguish from Dissuade & Confront Elder: Secure changes the elder's ENVIRONMENT rather than their mind; distinguish from Stop Disengage, which ends a specific payment stream rather than establishing ongoing control.
  - Secure Funds & Devices (former Informal Security Work code): Restricting the elder's exposure through financial or technical controls: cutting off or separating finances, withholding funds, payment controls, or blocking devices/network access (e.g., router blocking). Distinguish from Coordinate: these are structural restrictions on money and devices, not persuasion or supervision. Distinguish from Recover: Secure prevents further loss, Recover claws back money already lost.
  - Care & Health Protection (former Informal Security Work code): Protective work aimed at the elder's body and wellbeing rather than their money: calling an ambulance, arranging emergency or ongoing medical care after 保健品/therapy scams, mental-health support, and emotional or practical family support in the aftermath. Distinguish from Coordinate: Care remediates harm the elder has already suffered — especially health harm — while Coordinate intervenes against the scam itself. Verifying medical claims is Check, not Care.

- Public Appeal & Collective Action (merges: Collective Victim Action; Public Appeal & Advocacy):
  - Collective Victim Action (former Response code): Victims or their families act AS A GROUP: forming victim groups, organizing fellow victims, seeking witnesses, mass-submitting case materials, or pursuing recovery through collective protest — the 维权 repertoire of large Chinese investment/fundraising collapses. Distinguish from Report To Authorities & Legal Action and Seek Remedy From Seller by its organized, collective character; a label naming both an individual channel and collective organizing takes both codes.
  - Public Appeal & Advocacy (former Informal Security Work code): Appealing to the public, media, or society for help, attention, or justice on behalf of a victim, and organized victim-advocacy/rights-defense (维权) activity, including anti-MLM (反传销) rescue interventions. Distinguish from Warn: an appeal seeks help or justice for an existing victim, Warn alerts potential future victims. Distinguish from Legal: 维权 campaigning is extra-judicial pressure; filed lawsuits are Legal.

- Accept Comply: The elder (or occasionally the household) went along with the scam at least once: believed the pitch, purchased, invested, paid fees, handed over cash or valuables, or underwent the promoted treatment. In sequential narratives ("paid, then reported") this captures the initial compliance phase before any later remedial action. Distinguish from Continue Persist In Belief, which requires persisting AFTER red flags surfaced or family objected.

- Continue Persist In Belief: The elder keeps believing in or engaging with the scam despite family opposition, warnings, or emerging evidence — or a previously skeptical family member wavers back toward belief. Distinguish from Accept Comply (one-time or initial compliance): Continue requires persistence AGAINST pushback or red flags. Private persistence only; recruiting others is Promote Advocate Scheme.

- Promote Advocate Scheme: The poster or the elder actively promotes, endorses, or recruits for the scam product or scheme — evangelism rather than resistance. Covers deeply-converted victims who become advocates and posts written by promoters themselves. Distinguish from Continue Persist In Belief: promotion adds outward recruitment or advertising on top of private belief.

- Reject Refuse: The target — the elder, or more often a family member being recruited through the elder — refuses the offer, resists or evades recruitment (typically into 传销), declines to hand over money, or flees the scheme's control. Distinguish from Dissuade & Confront Elder: Reject is refusing a demand made of ONESELF; distinguish from Stop Disengage, which requires prior engagement that is then broken off.

- Stop Disengage: Breaking off an engagement already underway: halting further payments or treatment, canceling an order, cutting off funding, or pulling invested money out. Actor may be the elder or the family cutting off the money flow. Distinguish from Reject Refuse (never engaged) and from Seek Remedy From Seller: Stop is unilateral cessation, Remedy asks the counterparty to give money back.

- Distress & Regret: Emotional-state responses without (or overshadowing) instrumental action: severe emotional distress, post-purchase regret, or scam-induced family strain intense enough to threaten relationships. Distress that merely accompanies an action (e.g., a tearful refund demand) is coded under the action, not here.

- Other Respond: Residual for responses fitting no victim-, family-, or community-side code — for example, a perpetrator-side response such as a self-identified scammer's public confession claiming to have repented and abandoned fraud. Do not force such posts into Stop Disengage, whose actor is a victim or family member.

- N/A: Leave this variable N/A when the post narrates no response and no protective, investigative, remedial, or accountability action by anyone — the elder, the family, fellow victims, the community, media, or institutions — for example, bare news reposts or link shares and pure scheme descriptions in which no one's reaction to the scam is recorded. Note that merely posting to ask the forum for judgment is itself Seek Help & Advice, so pure help-seeking posts are not N/A. Use N/A rather than inferring a response the text does not state.

9. One-Off vs. Repeated (single-select)

- One-Off
- Repeated
- N/A

Coding instruction: Select exactly one code. This is a binary determination about the number of extraction events over time — do not use compounds. Note that unlike the Reddit codebook there is no "unspecified" value here: when the post describes a scam episode, commit to One-Off or Repeated.

Note: The criterion is the count of extraction events, not the duration of a single transaction. A lengthy one-time negotiation (e.g., an hours-long sales pitch ending in one purchase) is still One-Off; a second purchase, a top-up, or any recurring charge flips it to Repeated.

Definitions:

- One-Off: The scam involved a single extraction event — one purchase, one payment, one transfer, or one signed contract — with no recurrence described. Typical cases: a single overpriced purchase (tour-stop jewelry, street-vendor goods, TV-ad orders), one 存款变保单 insurance signing, one phone-call transfer. Distinguish from Repeated: if the post mentions even a second extraction event or an ongoing charging mechanism, code Repeated instead.

- Repeated: The victim was extracted from multiple times or on an ongoing basis — repeated purchases, recurring charges, escalating deposits, or sustained involvement in a scheme. Typical cases: 保健品 seminar cycles, 传销 participation, repeated investment top-ups, recurring unauthorized billing (吸费). Requires at least two distinct extraction events or a continuing extraction mechanism (subscription, ongoing scheme). Distinguish from One-Off: continuity or recurrence of extraction is the deciding feature, not how long a single transaction took.

- N/A: Leave N/A when the post contains no scam episode with an identifiable extraction (or attempted extraction) whose recurrence could be judged — e.g., abstract discussion threads (碰瓷/扶不扶 debates), general PSA content with no concrete victim episode. Do not use N/A to dodge a hard binary call on an actual incident.

10. Party Intervened (single or up to two; N/A when no third party appears)

- Police Response
- Government, Judicial, Regulatory & Legal Aid
- Bank Intervention
- Insurer Response
- Seller / Company Response
- Telecom Carrier Response
- Platform Moderation
- Media Exposure & Investigation
- Medical & Care Institutions
- N/A

Code every third-party actor the episode names, up to two. Add a second code only when two distinct actors intervene; never code more than two. Do not force a code when no third-party actor appears.

Note: Keep the counterparty business (Seller / Company Response) apart from the marketplace hosting it (Platform Moderation) and from the billing carrier (Telecom Carrier Response), and keep the deposit bank (Bank Intervention) apart from the insurer whose policy is disputed (Insurer Response) — bancassurance 存款变保单 compounds take both. The two merged codes group the former codes listed under them; the constituent definitions are retained verbatim below as coding guidance.

Definitions:

- Police Response (merges the former police outcome ladder: Police Enforcement Action; Police Investigation / Response; Police Declined / No Effective Action):
  - Police Enforcement Action: Police took a concrete coercive or remedial step against the scammers — arrest or detention of suspects or ringleaders, recovery of funds, or coercive measures such as travel restrictions on executives. Distinguish from Police Investigation / Response: this code requires a stated arrest, recovery, or other coercive outcome, not mere police engagement.
  - Police Investigation / Response: Police were contacted, attended the scene, received complaints, or opened or conducted an investigation, with no stated arrest, recovery, or refusal — the middle tier of the police outcome ladder. Includes generic "报警/police intervened" mentions and police appearing alongside another actor without a failure qualifier.
  - Police Declined / No Effective Action: Police were approached but declined to act, referred the case elsewhere, cited insufficient evidence, or their involvement produced no remedy. Distinguish from Police Investigation / Response: an explicit negative outcome ("不管", refused, no effective action) must be stated, not merely no result yet.

- Government, Judicial, Regulatory & Legal Aid (merges: Judicial Process; Market & Consumer Regulators; Government & Community Authorities; Civil Society & Legal Aid):
  - Judicial Process: Courts, prosecutors (检察院), arbitration bodies, or lawyers were engaged in the case, whatever the outcome — conviction and imprisonment as much as a dismissed claim. Distinguish from Civil Society & Legal Aid, which covers non-state legal-aid services and volunteers rather than the formal legal system.
  - Market & Consumer Regulators: Consumer-protection and market-supervision bodies — the 12315 hotline, 工商/market regulator, drug regulator, consumer associations, industry associations — were engaged, with outcomes ranging from investigation to refusal or an unreachable hotline. Distinguish from Government & Community Authorities: this code needs a named specialized watchdog with a consumer-protection mandate, not generic 政府.
  - Government & Community Authorities: Generic government bodies or grassroots governance organs — ministries, local authorities, 村委会, 乡镇/社区 staff, or unnamed "authorities" — were involved, notified, or urged to act. Appeals demanding intervention count even when no action has yet been delivered. Distinguish from the police codes, Market & Consumer Regulators, and Judicial Process, which take named specialized bodies.
  - Civil Society & Legal Aid: Non-state helpers — 反传销 (anti-MLM) volunteer rescuers or legal-aid services — stepped in, typically where formal institutions stalled. Distinguish from Judicial Process: these are non-state, non-commercial actors, not courts or retained lawyers.

- Bank Intervention: A bank acted, or refused to act, on the victim's accounts or funds — blocking a withdrawal or transfer, returning principal, processing a cancellation, paying token compensation, or refusing resolution. Distinguish from Insurer Response: the bank is the deposit/payment institution; when a 存款变保单 label names both bank and insurer, code both.

- Insurer Response: An insurance company responded to the dispute over a mis-sold policy or claim — denying a claim, refusing a full refund or withdrawal, offering a premium refund, failing to resolve, or responding with no stated outcome. Distinguish from Bank Intervention: the actor here is the insurer handling the policy at issue.

- Seller / Company Response: The selling business itself (or an implicated/impersonated company) responded to the complaint — refunding, refusing a refund or return, denying allegations or affiliation, deflecting through customer service, or disappearing outright. Distinguish from Platform Moderation: this is the accused counterparty, not the marketplace hosting it.

- Telecom Carrier Response: A mobile/telecom carrier or its customer service handled the dispute over charges or services — confirming deductions, refusing service, leaving a complaint pending, or declaring itself unable to resolve. Distinguish from Seller / Company Response: the actor is the operator whose billing or line service is at issue.

- Platform Moderation: An e-commerce or content platform acted, or was urged to act, on scam content or transactions it hosts — customer-service intervention, limiting recommendations, official denials, or user appeals to ban a streamer. "Urged to ban" counts as platform-directed intervention even though the action is only demanded. Distinguish from Seller / Company Response: the platform is the intermediary, not the seller.

- Media Exposure & Investigation: News media (newspapers, TV stations) investigated, exposed, or warned about the scam, sometimes jointly with regulators or witnesses. A poster's appeal to media that produced no media action does not qualify. Distinguish from Market & Consumer Regulators: journalism, not a state watchdog — when both act, code both.

- Medical & Care Institutions: Hospitals, ambulance services, or nursing-home departments intervened in the aftermath — diagnosing or treating scam-related health harm, remedying botched services, or being notified about the incident. Distinct from all remedy-seeking codes: the intervention targets health or care harm rather than money.

- N/A: Leave this variable N/A when no third-party institution, business, or organized actor appears in the episode — the poster describes, warns about, or asks about the scam without any bank, police, regulator, company, platform, media outlet, or care institution having been contacted or having acted. Do not force a code onto a purely family-internal or advice-seeking narrative.

11. Emotional Frame (one or two dominant frames; never more than two)

- Anger, Outrage & Injustice
- Protective Concern & Sympathy
- Warning & Caution
- Frustration & Exasperation
- Helplessness & Desperation
- Suspicion & Distrust
- Fear, Alarm & Urgency
- Promotional Appeal
- Grief, Hurt & Regret
- Caregiver Strain & Distress
- Ridicule & Sarcasm
- Relief & Vindication
- Help-Seeking

Code the one or two dominant affective stances of the post; never code more than two. Every post gets at least one frame — this variable is never left empty.

Note: Several codes are deliberately close siblings and must be split by appraisal, not by intensity. Anger, Outrage & Injustice is moral heat at a wrongdoer, including the explicit claim of being wronged and the demand for redress; Frustration & Exasperation is worn-down irritation at a blocked goal (a parent who won't listen, a hotline that stonewalls) — the two routinely co-occur but are distinct. Protective Concern & Sympathy is worry for victims; Warning & Caution is outward-facing alerting of the community; Fear, Alarm & Urgency is acute dread or now-or-never time pressure rather than diffuse worry. Helplessness & Desperation is perceived loss of efficacy; Caregiver Strain & Distress is the accumulated burden on the poster and household. Merged codes group the former frames listed under them; the constituent definitions are retained verbatim below as coding guidance.

Definitions:

- Anger, Outrage & Injustice (merges: Anger & Outrage; Justice & Injustice):
  - Anger & Outrage: Hot moral anger aimed at scammers, complicit companies, or institutions — indignation, resentment, and condemnation over the deception of elders. Distinguish from Frustration & Exasperation: anger targets a culprit; frustration is exasperation at blocked redress or an unpersuadable parent. Distinguish from Justice & Injustice, which adds a claim of being wronged and a demand for redress on top of the emotion.
  - Justice & Injustice: A wronged-party framing: naming what happened as an injustice or persecution and demanding accountability, redress, or punishment — 维权 posts, defiance, appeals to regulators and media. Distinguish from Anger & Outrage: justice labels assert a wrong and a claim to redress, not merely hot emotion (the two typically co-occur, each coded separately).

- Protective Concern & Sympathy (merges: Protective Concern; Empathy & Sympathy):
  - Protective Concern: Worry on behalf of an at-risk elder — usually the poster's own parent or grandparent — framed as wanting to shield or rescue them from an ongoing or looming scam; includes rescue-resolve ("determination") toward a brainwashed family member. Distinguish from Warning & Caution: concern is directed at one's own elder, warning at the public. Distinguish from Fear & Alarm (acute dread, not diffuse worry) and from Caregiver Strain & Distress (the toll on the poster, not worry for the elder).
  - Empathy & Sympathy: Compassion for scam victims other than (or beyond) one's own family — feeling for the defrauded elders described in the post. Distinguish from Protective Concern: empathy is other-oriented fellow-feeling toward victims in general; concern is protective worry about a specific elder the poster is responsible for.

- Warning & Caution: An alerting, admonitory stance: the post is framed as a caution to other readers — "beware of this scheme" — or expresses settled watchful wariness, from neutral PSA reposts to intensified public warnings. Distinguish from Protective Concern (outward-facing vs. inward-facing) and from Doubt & Suspicion: caution is a settled wary stance, suspicion is unresolved questioning of whether something is a scam.

- Frustration & Exasperation: Worn-down exasperation and irritation — at a parent who will not stop buying 保健品, at companies and hotlines that stonewall complaints, at scams that keep recurring. This includes adult children venting after failed persuasion or refund attempts. Distinguish from Anger & Outrage (moral indignation at a culprit) and from Helplessness & Desperation, where the poster has given up on efficacy altogether.

- Helplessness & Desperation: Powerlessness and despair: the poster feels unable to stop the scam, recover the money, or get through to the victim, and is at the end of their rope — parents irretrievably committed to a 传销 scheme, savings gone with no recourse. Distinguish from Frustration & Exasperation (still trying but thwarted vs. no efficacy left) and from Caregiver Strain & Distress (perceived impossibility vs. accumulated burden). Panic codes as Fear & Alarm, not here.

- Suspicion & Distrust (merges: Doubt & Suspicion; Betrayal & Distrust):
  - Doubt & Suspicion: Epistemic uncertainty: suspecting an offer or product is a scam, confusion about what actually happened, or stunned incredulity at a scheme — the whole "求鉴定 / is this a scam?" family, including bafflement at a parent's purchase. Distinguish from Betrayal & Distrust (post-confirmation loss of trust) and from Warning & Caution (settled wariness rather than open questioning).
  - Betrayal & Distrust: The rupture of trust: feeling betrayed by a trusted party — bank staff, insurers, phone carriers, relatives, longtime friends — and a resulting generalized loss of trust in people or institutions (存款变保单 posts are the archetype). Distinguish from Doubt & Suspicion: distrust is a post-hoc verdict after the deception is confirmed; suspicion is pre-verdict uncertainty.

- Fear, Alarm & Urgency (merges: Fear & Alarm; Urgency):
  - Fear & Alarm: Acute dread, anxiety, alarm, or panic about what the scam has done or may do — fear of financial ruin, health damage, a 碰瓷 setup, or an unfolding fraud escalating. Distinguish from Protective Concern (diffuse other-directed worry vs. acute dread) and from Urgency (time pressure to act, which need not involve dread).
  - Urgency: A now-or-never framing: the poster stresses that the scam is happening right now and immediate action — intervention, refunds, warnings, arrests — is needed. Distinguish from Fear & Alarm: urgency is time pressure to act, not dread of outcomes. It is a tone that can accompany warnings, anger, or help requests alike.

- Promotional Appeal: A persuasive, selling or defending register: hope, enthusiasm, reassurance, gratitude, and miracle/wealth appeals used to promote a product, company, treatment, or opportunity — often the scam itself (shill or testimonial posts), or a pitch riding on a warning frame. Distinguish from Relief & Vindication (product praise vs. resolution of one's own ordeal); hybrid "warning with promotion" posts carry both this code and Warning & Caution.

- Grief, Hurt & Regret (merges: Grief & Hurt; Regret & Moral Reflection):
  - Grief & Hurt: Sorrow, mourning, and emotional wounds: grieving a death or devastating loss connected to the scam, or deep relational hurt within the family over the deception. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (loss-focused sorrow vs. lack of efficacy); "hurt" — wounded feelings between kin — sits here rather than with anger.
  - Regret & Moral Reflection: Self-directed moral emotion: regret or embarrassment over having been fooled or having failed to intervene, remorse, retrospective self-examination, and moral dilemmas about the right thing to do (e.g., whether to tell a scammed mother the truth). Distinguish from Grief & Hurt (self-blame and moral conflict vs. sorrow over loss) and from Doubt & Suspicion (moral uncertainty about the right action vs. factual uncertainty about whether it is a scam).

- Caregiver Strain & Distress: The emotional and family toll of the scam on the poster and household: distress, family conflict, burnout, and exhaustion from managing a scammed elder. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (accumulated burden vs. perceived impossibility) and from Protective Concern (toll on the poster vs. worry for the elder).

- Ridicule & Sarcasm: A mocking, satirical, or sarcastic register: deriding scammers, gullible buying, or the absurdity of a scheme, including satire written in a scammer's voice (e.g., a mock 悔过书). Distinguish from Anger & Outrage: contemptuous humor, not hot indignation. A sarcastic warning carries both this code and Warning & Caution.

- Relief & Vindication: Positive resolution feelings: relief that a scam was stopped or losses recovered, and vindication at being proven right after a long fight. Distinguish from Justice & Injustice (redress achieved vs. redress demanded) and from Promotional Appeal (resolution of one's own ordeal vs. product praise).

- Help-Seeking: The post's affective register is a plea: the poster frames their situation as a distress-toned request for assistance, advice, or intervention from the community. Distinguish from Helplessness & Desperation (a plea directed at readers vs. an expression of powerlessness); this is the emotional register, not the Help & Advice Seeking post genre.

12. Loss Recovery Status (single-select)

- No (Not Recovered)
- Unknown (Outcome Unstated)
- Partial
- Prevented
- Yes (Recovered)
- N/A

Code exactly one value per post; this variable is never multi-coded. Use Partial, Prevented, or Yes only when the post gives explicit outcome evidence.

Note: this variable is shared with the Reddit codebook — both platforms use this identical value set.

Note: The confusable pair is No vs. Unknown. No requires positive evidence that the loss stands (a refusal to refund, an unreachable seller, a collapsed scheme, money written off); mere silence about the aftermath is Unknown. Separately, Prevented is not a recovery at all — the extraction never completed — so do not code Yes when family, bank, or police stopped the scam before money left.

Definitions:

- No (Not Recovered): The post gives positive evidence that the loss stands: money or property was not returned, and no successful recovery is described (refund refused, seller unreachable, scheme collapsed, loss written off). Distinguish from Unknown (Outcome Unstated): if the post simply never says what happened afterward, code Unknown. Distinguish from Partial: even a small refund moves the case to Partial.

- Unknown (Outcome Unstated): The post does not reveal whether the loss was recovered — the outcome is unstated, or the case is still open and unresolved at posting time. This includes seeking-help posts written mid-incident, warnings that end before any resolution, and third-party stories with no follow-up. Also code here assertions that the money came back that are unverified and may themselves be part of a scheme (the former "Claimed Recovered (Unverified)" code, folded in below):
  - Claimed Recovered (Unverified) (folded into Unknown): Someone in the post asserts the money came back, but the claim is unverified and may itself be part of a scheme — e.g., a victim repeating a Ponzi "you got your principal back" narrative, or a recovery-service promoter touting its own success. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): the coder records the assertion without endorsing it; the "recovery" may be scheme-internal paper gains or promotional puffery.

- Partial: Some but not all of the extracted value came back — a partial refund after negotiation, surrender value on a mis-sold policy at a loss, or return of a fraction of deposits from a collapsed scheme. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): recovery is materially incomplete. Distinguish from No (Not Recovered): at least something was returned.

- Prevented: The attempted scam was intercepted before the loss was completed — a relative, bank, police, or the target themself stopped it — so no loss existed to recover. Includes blocked transfers, fraudulent loan applications caught in time, and purchases stopped by family. Distinguish from Yes (Recovered): nothing was ever recovered because extraction never completed; this records successful prevention as its own analytically distinct outcome.

- Yes (Recovered): The post credibly reports that the loss was returned in full or essentially in full — a refund obtained, money returned after complaint or consumer-protection intervention, goods reversed. Distinguish from Partial (only some funds back), from Unknown (recovery merely asserted, not verified), and from Prevented (the loss never completed).

- N/A: Leave this variable N/A when the post contains no extraction episode whose outcome could be tracked — general discussion or debate posts (e.g., 碰瓷/扶老人 ethics threads), complaints with no monetary loss to the elder, or PSA/科普 content that never narrates a specific victim's loss. Do not force a code onto a post that has no loss in it.


Coding notes

- Variable 1 discipline: Scam Relevance is coded even when other variables are hard to fill; a post coded "No (Not a Scam)" or "Unknown (Undetermined)" can still carry purpose, category, and emotion codes describing the suspected episode.
- 5A vs 5B distinction: 5A records the channel through which the scheme first reached the elder (the X in "X to Y"); 5B records what the elder was brought to do (the Y). Always code one of each when the route is stated.
- 7 (Red Flags) vs 3 (Category): category is the scheme type; red flags are the warning signs the post itself evidences. Do not derive red flags from the category alone — each coded red flag needs support in the text.
- 11 (Emotional Frame): code the one or two DOMINANT frames of the poster (not of commenters); never more than two.
- Multi-select fields: 3 (up to 2), 4 (up to 3), 5A (up to 2), 5B (up to 3), 5 combined (up to 4), 6 (up to 2), 7 (up to 5), 8 (up to 4), 10 (up to 2). Do not force extra codes to fill a quota — code what the post evidences.
- N/A discipline: for 10 (Party Intervened), use N/A when no third-party institution appears. Never leave a variable blank — output "N/A" instead.

Output format

For the post you are given, output ONE JSON object, no other text, with EXACTLY these 12 keys. Every value is an array of code names copied verbatim from this codebook (single-select variables get a one-element array; use ["N/A"] where the variable does not apply):

{"scam": [...], "purpose_of_post": [...], "category_of_scam": [...], "promise_or_need": [...], "contact_to_commitment": [...], "asset_extracted": [...], "red_flag": [...], "respond": [...], "one_off_or_repeated": [...], "party_intervened": [...], "emotional_frame": [...], "loss_recovered": [...]}

For "contact_to_commitment", put the 5A (channel) and 5B (commitment) codes together in the one array.
